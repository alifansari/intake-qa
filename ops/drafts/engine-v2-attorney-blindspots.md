> **Framing rule for everything below:** the attorney's goals (net fee/hour, catastrophic-risk

## 1. The six documented triage errors

### (a) Base-rate neglect / overconfidence on vivid features
**Evidence [sourced]:** Goodman-Delahunty, Granhag, Hartwig & Loftus (2010, *Psychology, Public Policy & Law*, n=481 attorneys): lawyers systematically overconfident about their own case outcomes; **the higher the expressed confidence, the more likely they fell short**; calibration did NOT improve with experience, and prompting lawyers to generate reasons-they-might-fail didn't fix it. Kiser/Asher/McShane (2008, *JELS*, 2,054 cases) puts a dollar magnitude on miscalibrated case valuation: plaintiffs' side committed decision error in **61%** of rejected-settlement cases, averaging **$43k worse** than the last offer. **Countermeasure:** every score ships with the firm's own boring prior rendered as a sentence — "Soft-tissue MIST rear-ender, minimal-limits carrier: at this firm, median net fee $X, N=Y, Z% became dogs." The vivid feature stays visible; the prior stands next to it. Never "decline this"; always "cases like this here did that."

### (b) Availability cascade after a big verdict
**Evidence [sourced-adjacent]:** the mechanism is documented at industry scale — the nuclear-verdict → attorney-advertising → filing-surge loop is described by DRI/Travelers/insurers ($2.5B plaintiff ad spend in 2024 chasing verdict lookalikes); anchoring research shows one salient number distorts professional valuation (mock damages 823% higher under a $5M vs $250k anchor). Firm-level "signed the next 10 lookalikes" is **[inference]** from these plus availability-heuristic basics — no study measures intra-firm intake drift post-verdict. Honest gap. **Countermeasure:** a *recency-contrast diagnostic*: when the last 60 days' signings in a case type deviate >2σ from the firm's trailing-year mix, surface it — "you've signed 8 dog-bite cases since the Alvarez verdict; trailing-year base rate was 2/quarter, median net fee $X." Names the cascade without vetoing it (maybe the verdict genuinely changed local settlement values — attorney's call).

### (c) Sunk-cost escalation in the develop queue
**Evidence [sourced]:** escalation-of-commitment is one of the most replicated JDM findings (Staw line); legal-domain confirmation exists (JELS sunk-cost/settlement work; unrecoverable fees are the canonical legal sunk cost — practitioner literature treats it as endemic). The specific claim that a *develop track becomes a commitment device* is **[inference]** — but it's structurally predictable: each records-request invested makes decline feel like waste. **Countermeasure:** develop-track entries get a **pre-registered exit condition at entry** ("developing to learn: limits. If limits < $50k, expected disposition: decline") plus a hard review date. At review, the engine restates the *original* question and shows only new information — the invested hours are deliberately not displayed. Information-gathering becomes escalation the moment a case survives its own pre-registered kill criterion; the engine's job is to remember the criterion the attorney set.

### (d) Occasion noise (time-of-day / caseload / mood)
**Evidence [sourced]:** no direct intra-attorney intake-reliability study exists (gap worth stating). But adjacent expert domains are damning: Kahneman's insurance noise audits — same file, same company — found **median 55% divergence between underwriters and 43% between claims adjusters** where executives predicted ~10%; radiologists show **~20–32% intra-observer discrepancy against their own prior reads**; hungry-judge parole data (Danziger — contested, note the Weinshall-Margel critique) and Chen et al.'s asylum-judge sequencing effects (*QJE* 2016) show ordering/occasion artifacts in high-stakes legal judgment. There is no reason a solo attorney triaging call #9 at 6pm is the exception. **Magnitude estimate [inference]:** if adjusters hit 43% on structured files, attorney intake noise on phone narratives is plausibly worse. **Countermeasure:** this is the engine's cleanest value-add and it's already the Grove point — **the score is the noise-free second reader**, identical at 9am and 6pm. Additionally: log score-vs-attorney divergence *by time-of-day and open-caseload* and show the attorney their own occasion pattern quarterly. Nobody has ever shown a PI attorney their own noise audit.

### (e) Selection-on-signed feedback (the wicked environment)
**Evidence [sourced]:** Hogarth's kind/wicked framework (Hogarth, Lejarraga & Soyer 2015) is exactly this: declined cases exit the attorney's information environment forever, so the decline threshold **can never self-correct** — confidence grows without skill (Einhorn's classic point about selection feedback). The attorney sees every dog they signed (pushing thresholds up) and no gem they declined (nothing pushing back down). Predictable direction of miscalibration: **over-decline at the margin, asymmetrically** — yet loss-aversion on dogs is also real, so the net direction is firm-specific and unmeasured **[inference]**. **Countermeasure:** a *declined-case outcome loop* — lightweight tracking of a sample of declines (referred-out cases via the referral fee itself; otherwise periodic docket/settlement checks where feasible, or simply the referral partner's report). Even 20 tracked declines/year converts a wicked environment to a semi-kind one. This is something no PI firm does and every funder does (they audit passed deals).

### (f) Delegation drift (criteria decay through intake staff)
**Evidence [sourced for the failure, inference for the mechanism]:** the intake layer demonstrably degrades firm intent — Clio's mystery-shopper work: ~half of firms don't answer; ~35% of inquiries never get a response; average conversion ~14%; mystery-shopping vendors exist precisely because staff drift from standards. That staff apply a *degraded copy of the attorney's selection criteria* (vs. merely dropping balls) is **[inference]** — but it's the firm's own product thesis, and the noise data in (d) applies doubly to a non-attorney applying someone else's rubric. **Countermeasure:** the rubric IS the anti-drift artifact — staff collect structured fields; the engine applies the attorney's criteria identically on call #1 and #500. The Track A/Track B wall matters here: measure staff on *extraction completeness*, never on score outcomes.

## 2. What carriers and funders do that a 2-attorney PI firm doesn't

**Transfers to a 2-attorney firm:**
- **Structured feature extraction at first notice** (Colossus: ~600 injury codes, valuation against a claims database — the carrier across the table is *already scoring this case mechanically*; the asymmetry is the pitch). Transfers fully: a fixed extraction schema costs nothing at small scale.
- **Early-severity triage routing** (Milliman/Guidewire claims triage: score at FNOL, route complex-early; the hard cases are the innocuous-looking ones that escalate). Transfers as the two-sided alert.
- **Explicit decline documentation** (funders document every pass and why; also malpractice-defensive — ABA Profile: plaintiff PI is the #1 claims source and "unworthy client" representation drives ~two-thirds of catastrophic payouts). Transfers fully, near-zero cost.
- **Staged diligence with budget-to-value ratios** (Burford: budget-to-settlement-value ratio as a gate; Omni Bridgeway: damages ≥ ~10× investment, collectability first-class). Transfers as ratio thinking: expected net fee ÷ expected carry cost, with coverage as a gate not a term.
- **Pre-registered exit conditions** during diligence. Transfers as the develop-track fix (1c).

**Does NOT transfer:**
- Outcome-calibrated regression models — carriers have millions of claims; a 2-attorney firm has dozens/year. Dawes unit-weighting is the correct small-N substitute; don't cosplay Colossus.
- Portfolio-level diversification math — funders diversify across hundreds of uncorrelated cases; a small firm's "portfolio" is 30–80 files where one mass disaster is un-diversifiable. What transfers is only the *cash-flow ladder* discipline (mix of fast-settling and long-tail files) **[inference]**.
- Adverse-selection pricing. Firms can't price; they can only take/decline/refer.

## 3. The option-value blind spot

Attorneys under-use the two instruments that preserve optionality. **Refer-out:** standard referral fees run 25–33% of the earned fee (CA-compliant under CRPC 1.5.1 with written client consent) — a case worth $300k in a specialist's hands yields ~$25–33k referred for near-zero hours, plausibly the firm's **highest fee-per-hour transaction**, yet ego/optimism (the Goodman-Delahunty overconfidence result applies directly: "I can handle this") keeps marginal-fit cases in-house **[inference — no study quantifies forgone referral fees; the economics are arithmetic]**. **Develop-then-decide:** underused in one direction (binary take/decline on thin data) and abused in the other (1c's escalation). **Countermeasure:** the engine prices the options explicitly — every score in the marginal band shows three comparisons: expected net fee/hour *kept*, expected referral outcome *referred* (a fee for near-zero hours), expected value-of-information *developed* (what the missing fact would change, at what cost). Referral stops being an ego event and becomes a line item. This also compounds the referral-network reputation goal: a firm known for referring the right cases up gets the down-referrals back. (Compliance note: the ENGINE never computes dollar referral figures at intake — tier-based comparisons only, per the standing no-dollars-at-intake rule; the dollar arithmetic here is design rationale, not output.)

## 4. Honest nulls — where the engine defers

1. **Live credibility read — partially.** Bond & DePaulo (2006, 206 studies): humans detect deception at **54%**, professionals ~56% — the attorney's *lie detector* is near-chance and the engine shouldn't defer to it. But the attorney's read of **how this client presents to a jury/adjuster** (likability, articulateness, consistency under questioning) is a genuine unscoreable input — that's presentation-quality assessment, not lie detection, and it survives contact with the literature. Engine: takes "client presentation: strong/weak" as an attorney-supplied *input*, never generates it.
2. **Local venue/jury/adjuster knowledge.** Which carrier's local office lowballs, which defense firm folds, this judge's Howell rulings — real, unpublished, high-value knowledge. Encodable eventually (it's just data the engine hasn't seen); defer until N exists.
3. **Broken-leg exceptions (Meehl's own carve-out).** A decisive particular the rubric has no variable for — the defendant is the client's employer's biggest customer; the client is dying. The override channel exists for exactly this; the discipline is *logging* overrides with reasons and grading them later, which converts broken legs into either vindicated judgment or new rubric variables.
4. **Case-chemistry / client-management forecast.** Whether *this attorney* can work with *this client* for 18 months — no engine sees this; a difficult client is a legitimate decline reason the rubric shouldn't launder into a quality score.

The adoption-honest position: the engine wins on **consistency, priors, memory, and option-pricing**; the attorney wins on **presentation reads, local knowledge, broken legs, and chemistry**. The engine that claims the second list doesn't ship.
