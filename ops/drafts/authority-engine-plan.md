## 0. The thesis in one paragraph

The independent-scorer moat (insight 2026-07-07 D1) is not a feature — it is a *structural
position* that every incumbent is disqualified from holding, because a CRM, an answering
service, or an AI-voice vendor cannot grade a market it sells into without a conflict of
interest it can't escape without dismantling its own business model. Moody's, J.D. Power, and
Consumer Reports converted that same structural independence into durable pricing power by doing
three things a paid certifier never does: **publish the methodology, publish the error rate, and
refuse to be captured by the thing they rate.** This plan builds the one asset that does that —
an independent, CIPA-safe, Yang-signed mystery-shop benchmark of California PI intake — and it
designs the two pieces the existing drafts deferred: (1) an actual *instrument* (sampling frame,
scored rubric, confidence tiers) rather than a list of dimensions, and (2) a *published
false-alarm-rate methodology* rigorous enough that a skeptical managing partner, a journalist, or
opposing counsel can attack it and lose. One asset, four jobs: replaces self-interested vendor
stats with our citable number; earns the first meeting (B-001); carries the Spanish-gap PR
franchise (B-002); and instantiates the moat competitors structurally cannot copy.

**The one rule that governs everything below:** if we ever tune the finding to sell the product,
we have destroyed the only property that made it worth building. The safeguard is not good
intentions — it is **pre-registration** (§4.4): the analysis plan, the confidence thresholds, and
the false-alarm cut-offs are frozen and dated *before* any firm is dialed, exactly as a
registered clinical trial freezes its endpoints before enrollment. We cannot p-hack the market
into looking worse if we published the ruler first.

## PART A — THE INSTRUMENT (B-006)

The Yang protocol (`yang-cipa-632-mystery-shop-protocol.md`) settled the *legal posture*
(record nothing; scripted fictional shopper; paper rubric; no firm named). The report outline
settled the *narrative*. Neither specified the actual instrument. This part does.

### A.1 The sampling frame (the "who we call," made concrete)

**Population (target of inference).** California plaintiff-side personal-injury firms of **2–10
attorneys** that **visibly buy inbound lead flow** (Google LSA/PPC, TV, billboard, or Spanish
media) — i.e. firms for whom a leaked intake call has a hard, already-paid-for dollar cost
(insight B4). Solos with no paid intake volume and 50-attorney ad-machine firms are both out of
frame: the former have no leak worth scoring, the latter are not our buyer.

**Geographic strata — DECISION FLAGGED FOR ALI.** The 2026-07-07 docs frame this as *NorCal*;
the 2026-07-10 LACBA pivot went *LA/statewide*. **Recommendation: align benchmark geography to
wherever the founding cohort actually lands.** Given live LACBA outreach, the base case is
**Greater Los Angeles** (LA + Orange + Inland Empire metros), with a NorCal arm (SF–Oakland,
San Jose, Sacramento) added only if the founding cohort is NorCal. A benchmark whose geography
matches the firms Ali is actually selling is worth 10× one that doesn't. Pick one primary metro
before enumeration.

**Frame construction (ethical, public-source-only — reuse the `dream25-targeting-plan.md` SOP).**
Enumerate the frame from public directories with a **≥2-source verification rule per firm**:
State Bar member records, Justia/Avvo/Super Lawyers PI directories, and live Google **LSA
results** for `"car accident lawyer [city]"` (the LSA badge *is* the proof of paid intake
volume). For each enumerated firm record: firm name, public main line, attorney count band,
practice mix, **visible-Spanish-media presence (Y/N)** (Spanish landing page, Spanish LSA/TV,
listed bilingual intake). No firm PII beyond public business-directory data; no fabricated firms.

**Stratification.** Two axes: (1) metro, (2) **visible-Spanish-media presence** — because the
Spanish arm (A.5) must be powered, so we must oversample Spanish-advertising firms into the frame.

**Target N and honest power.** For a market-level proportion at 95% confidence:
- ±10 percentage-point margin → **~96 firms**
- ±7 pt margin → **~196 firms**
Given one-call-per-firm and founder-hours as the binding constraint (insight B1), the realistic
**pilot wave is N ≈ 60–100 firms**, drawn at random within strata from the enumerated frame,
reported honestly as a **±10–13 pt** market estimate — a probability sample, never a census, never
a convenience sample of firms we happened to know. State the frame size, the draw method, and the
response/reachability disposition of every drawn firm (answered / voicemail / service /
unreachable) so the denominator is auditable.

**Exclusions (pre-specified).** Referral-only shops and firms that don't take inbound PI intake;
any firm in the founding cohort, the active pipeline, or with a prior Ali relationship (conflict
of interest — the independent scorer cannot shop its own prospects); duplicate offices of the same
firm.

**Contact discipline (from the Yang protocol, restated).** One call per firm per wave. No repeat
dialing. No after-hours "does voicemail work" probing without a specific Yang-approved reason.
This is the harassment/capping guardrail (protocol Q6, G4).

### A.2 The fixed 4-minute scenario (locked, so the study measures the firm not the shopper)

Adopt the protocol §1 persona verbatim: an adult **passenger** in a rear-end MVA ~two weeks ago,
one doctor visit, neck/back soreness, "not sure it's worth a lawyer," unsigned, "just calling
around." Low-drama, non-emergency, non-manipulative by design. Fixed disclosures only (first name,
study-controlled callback number, city, timing, the one visit). Hard stops: never claims a real
case, never provides third-party PII, never proceeds toward retaining or e-signing, declines
appointments politely and ends the call. Candor rule for "are you a tester?" is the open question
Yang must resolve (protocol Q5) — **not settled here.**

### A.3 The scored rubric (a real instrument, not a list of dimensions)

Eight items, each scored as a **binary observable** (did the firm do the thing / not) plus four
**timing metrics**. Binary-observable is deliberate: it is the format with the highest inter-rater
reliability under 4-minute, no-playback, memory-plus-notes conditions, and it is robust to the
one thing we refuse to capture — the verbatim words. We are **not** scoring an "empathy meter"
(letter §V; Forsberg 2007 reliability caveat); we are scoring whether concrete, checkable intake
*behaviors* occurred.

| # | Item (scored about the FIRM) | Observable | Grounding |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Reached a human capable of intake** | live human / IVR / answering service / voicemail / unreached | Clio 2024 secret shopper: only **40%** of firms answered at all (VERIFIED, see A.6) |
| R2 | **Captured the core facts** (who/what/when — collision type, date, injury) | yes / partial / no | basic intake competence |
| R3 | **Asked about treatment** (have you seen a doctor / are you treating) | yes / no | the single most case-determining intake question |
| R4 | **Explained next steps** (what happens now, timeline, a named person/callback) | yes / no | Clio: only **36%** explained the process (VERIFIED) |
| R5 | **Offered a concrete follow-through** (booked callback / warm transfer to a decision-maker) | yes / no | speed-to-lead decay (insight: Lead-Response) |
| R6 | **Callback actually occurred** to the study-controlled line within a fixed follow-window (e.g. 1 business day), if a message was taken | yes / no / n-a | responsiveness, measured not asserted |
| R7 | **Spanish-capability handling** (Spanish arm only — see A.5) | competent Spanish intake / interpreter offered / dead-ended in English | B-002 tie-in; Fricker |
| R8 | **Professionalism/tone** (neutral coded observation, NOT a transcript) | ok / notable-negative | protocol §2, coded only |

**Timing metrics (recorded as numbers):** rings-to-answer; hold time; time-to-a-human-capable-
of-intake; callback latency (R6). No verbatim quotes are ever recorded (protocol §3 — the retained
artifact is an evaluation, not a surreptitious record).

The rubric is itself a Yang-reviewed deliverable (protocol G8). R7's Spanish sub-protocol and the
matched-pair design (A.5) get their own line in the sign-off.

### A.4 Tiered confidence (BI-RADS/Clawson-MPDS-style — precedent already in the DNA)

No probabilistic inference is dressed as certainty (compliance §IV; seed insight — BI-RADS,
Clawson MPDS). Each *market-level finding* (not each firm — we never publish a firm) is assigned a
tier, and the tier is a **function of the instrument's own measured operating characteristics**
(A.4 depends on A.4-the-methodology below):

- **Tier 1 — Reportable as a point estimate + CI.** Item inter-rater κ ≥ 0.75 **and** measured
  false-alarm rate ≤ 10% on the calibration set.
- **Tier 2 — Reportable as a directional range only.** κ 0.60–0.75 **or** false-alarm 10–20%.
  Reported as "roughly X–Y%," never a single number.
- **Tier 3 — Not reported as a number.** κ < 0.60 **or** false-alarm > 20%. Described
  qualitatively, or the item is dropped before the wave. An item we can't score reliably does not
  get a number in the report — that discipline *is* the credibility.

This means the confidence tier of every headline number is earned in calibration (A.4-method)
**before** fieldwork, and any item that can't clear Tier 2 is cut from the instrument, not
fudged in the writeup.

### A.4-method — THE PUBLISHED FALSE-ALARM-RATE METHODOLOGY (the crown jewel)

This is the piece no vendor study has and the piece the letter already stakes publicly. It is
also genuinely hard, because **we record nothing in the field** (the entire CIPA posture), so we
cannot re-adjudicate the real mystery-shop calls after the fact. The false-alarm rate therefore
**cannot** be estimated from the field calls. It must be estimated on a **separate calibration
set where the ground truth is known by construction** — and here the CIPA posture actually helps
us, because it creates a clean asymmetry Yang can bless:

> **Field calls: record nothing** (no firm consent → §632/§632.7 avoided by capturing nothing).
> **Calibration calls: record everything** (both parties are our own staff and all-party-consent
> — the one situation where CIPA is fully satisfied). Yang confirms the two-track design (add to
> protocol Q-list).

**Signal-detection framing.** For each rubric item, the instrument makes a call about whether a
behavior occurred. In a benchmark that *indicts a market*, the reputationally dangerous error is
the **false alarm** = scoring a firm "FAILED to do X" when it actually did X. That is the error
that would let a firm say "your study is wrong about me," and it is the error a market-shaming
report is structurally tempted to tolerate. So it is the one we measure and publish as the floor
of interpretation.

- **False-alarm (false-positive) rate** = FP / (FP + TN) = P(scored "failed X" | truly did X)
- **Miss (false-negative) rate** = FN / (FN + TP) = P(scored "did X" | truly didn't) — flatters
  the market, published too for symmetry.

**The calibration protocol (staged, recorded, ground-truth-by-construction):**

1. **Build ~24–40 calibration calls** — role-played intake calls where two staff play
   intake-worker and shopper. The intake-worker's script is written with a **factorial /
   Latin-square design** so each rubric behavior (R2–R5, R7) is *present* in a known subset of
   calls and *absent* in a known subset. Because we authored the intake side, the ground truth
   per item per call is known exactly. Include hard cases on purpose: partial captures,
   ambiguous "sort-of" next-steps, a Spanish call with an English hold, code-switching.
2. **Record them** (all-party internal consent, §632-clean).
3. **Have the actual field shoppers score them LIVE** under the identical 4-minute,
   no-playback, memory-plus-notes conditions they will use in the field — so we measure the
   *instrument-as-actually-used* (including the shopper-memory limitation), not an idealized
   paper rubric scored from a transcript. This is the critical realism control: a false-alarm
   rate measured off a leisurely transcript read would understate the real field error.
4. **Multiple shoppers score the same calibration calls** → compute per-item **inter-rater
   reliability** (Cohen's κ for two raters, Fleiss' κ for more). This feeds the Tier gate (A.4).
5. **Compute and publish**, per item and overall: false-alarm rate, miss rate, κ, each with a
   **Wilson-score 95% confidence interval** on the calibration N (small-N honest: an N≈30
   calibration set yields wide CIs, and we report them wide rather than pretend precision).

**How it appears in the report.** Every headline finding is stated *against its instrument's
own false-alarm floor*: e.g. "X% of sampled firms scored as not asking about treatment (Tier 1;
instrument false-alarm rate 6%, 95% CI 2–14% on 30 calibration calls)." A reader can subtract our
own error rate from our own claim. That single sentence is the difference between a vendor stat
and a rating.

**Stated limitations (load-bearing, not buried — compliance §VIII).** (a) Calibration actors
running a script are a *proxy* for live firms; scripted behavior may be easier or harder to score
than the real thing — stated as a limitation, and the factorial design deliberately includes
ambiguous cases to stress it. (b) The false-alarm rate is itself an estimate with a CI, not a
constant. (c) One-call-per-firm means R6 callback and any "the firm usually does better" variance
is unobserved — reported as a single-encounter snapshot, never as a firm's steady-state quality.

### A.5 The Spanish matched-pair arm (paired-testing audit science, not "we called in Spanish")

The Spanish finding is the highest-variance, most-defamable-if-wrong number in the whole
enterprise (outline §4). It gets the most rigorous design in the plan, borrowed wholesale from a
50-year-old, litigation-tested methodology:

**Paired-testing (audit) design.** This is the exact method HUD and fair-housing/employment
enforcement have used since the 1970s to measure differential treatment: send two testers who
differ on **one** attribute and hold everything else constant. Here: a subset of firms — those
with **visible Spanish-media presence** (where competent Spanish intake is a reasonable
expectation the firm itself advertises) — receive **both** an English-scenario call and a
Spanish-scenario call, on **different days**, with **different shopper voices**, running the
**identical fact pattern**. The measured quantity is the **within-firm differential** in rubric
handling (R2–R5, R7). Pairing within firm controls for firm-level quality and isolates the
language effect — a far stronger claim than comparing Spanish-called firms to English-called
firms across the sample.

**Why this is the defensible way to say a hard thing.** "Firm-controlled differential treatment
of an identical injury inquiry, by language" is a construct courts and regulators already
recognize from housing/employment audits — it converts the Spanish finding from anecdote into
audit evidence. Fricker's testimonial injustice (seed insight; the letter's coda) supplies the
*why* — the accented/Spanish-speaking caller absorbs a credibility deficit at the moment of
entrusting — and paired-testing supplies the *proof*.

**Guardrails (non-negotiable).** Spanish shoppers are our own scripted testers, never a real
Spanish-speaking claimant (§VI). Same no-record posture, no lower bar (protocol G7). The finding
is `[TO BE MEASURED]`; until measured it is "forthcoming," never quoted (outline §4). Ships only
with a confidence tier, a stated N, and Yang's sign-off. A justice claim with a shaky number is
worse than no claim.

- **Build now (cheap, no dialing, no hours-drain, no Yang-fieldwork-gate):** the sampling-frame
  method, the rubric, the tier scheme, the **calibration set + false-alarm methodology**, and the

  real calls from founding firms) becomes the *first* published dataset — it needs no mystery-shop

  is *not* independent — it's our own customers) structurally cannot be. Two datasets that agree
