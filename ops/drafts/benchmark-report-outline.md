## 0. The one-sentence thesis

**Northern California's plaintiff-PI bar has spent thirty years measuring the outcome of the
intake call and never once measuring the call — so we became the first independent party to
score it, published our own error rate, and here is what we found.**

The report is not a lead-gen asset wearing a study's clothes. It is a genuine, methodologically
published benchmark whose *form* is borrowed from J.D. Power / Moody's / a Consumer Reports test:
a fixed instrument, a defined sample, tiered confidence, and a stated false-alarm rate. The
business value falls out of the credibility, not the other way around. If we ever invert that —
tune the finding to sell the product — we have destroyed the only asset that made it worth
building. Say the number the data says, or don't publish.

## 2. The CIPA-safe methodology summary (this is what makes it citable — and it is a hard gate)

The methodology section is not an appendix; in an independent benchmark it is the product. It
must be written so a skeptical managing partner (or a journalist, or opposing counsel) can attack
it and lose. Summary of the protocol the report will describe:

1. **Fixed scenario, not live claimants.** A single standardized inbound scenario (a plausible,
   scripted mock injury inquiry) is used for every call. **No real claimant's call is ever
   scored for this report, and no live intake is intercepted.** This is what keeps us clear of
   CIPA §632 exposure entirely — there is no confidential communication of a real claimant being
   recorded (insight A3; letter §THE INVITATION already commits us to §632 discipline publicly).
2. **Four-minute rubric.** The behavioral rubric already described in the letter (MITI/OARS
   reflections and open-question ratio, ECCS empathic moves, Hobfoll PFA elements, SPIN
   sequencing, peak-end handling) — scored as *behavioral proxies, honestly labeled*, never as an
   "empathy meter" (letter §V; Forsberg 2007 empathy-reliability caveat carried over verbatim).
3. **Tiered confidence (BI-RADS-style).** Every finding carries a confidence tier; no
   probabilistic inference is dressed as certainty (compliance §IV; seed insight — BI-RADS /
   Clawson MPDS precedent).
4. **PUBLISHED false-alarm rate.** The report states, in its own numbers, how often our
   instrument mis-scores — the same wager the letter already stakes publicly. A benchmark that
   hides its own error rate is a vendor study; one that publishes it is a rating (§IV; Lizzeri).
5. **Yang-signed protocol BEFORE any dialing.** No call is placed until Roberta M. Yang signs the
   §632-safe fieldwork protocol. This is a **§II + §VII human-approval gate, not a formality.**

> ⛔ HARD GATE (§II / §VII): **No dialing, no mystery-shop call, no data collection of any kind
> begins before Yang signs the protocol.** This outline does not authorize fieldwork. It stages
> the shape of a report that can only be filled in after that sign-off. If anyone reads this and
> reaches for a phone, stop.

> COMPLIANCE NOTE (§II): Because the scenario is scripted and the "caller" is our own tester,
> the recording-consent problem is structural, not procedural — we control both ends of a mock
> call. Even so, Yang confirms the fixed-scenario design actually sits outside §632 before we
> rely on it. Do NOT treat my analysis as her sign-off.

## 4. How the Spanish-intake-gap chapter fits (the highest-variance play — handle with care)

The Spanish chapter (report §5) is the earned-PR engine (B-002) and it is also the one most
capable of blowing up if handled loosely. It fits like this:

- **Frame:** Fricker's testimonial injustice (seed insight; already the letter's CODA) — the
  accented or Spanish-speaking caller absorbs a credibility deficit at the exact moment of
  entrusting. It is a *justice* finding, not only a conversion finding. Only the independent
  scorer has the incentive to publish it (insight D3).
- **Anchors (VERIFIED, may cite with source):** ~28.8% of Californians speak Spanish at home
  (Census/ACS); SF-Oakland-Fremont metro ~43.4% speak a language other than English at home,
  Spanish-at-home ~15–17% (Census Reporter, ACS 2024). These are context, not findings.
- **The finding itself is `[TO BE MEASURED]`:** the delta between how the same fixed scenario is
  handled when presented by a Spanish-speaking tester vs. an English-speaking tester. This
  requires the protocol to include a matched Spanish-language arm — Yang signs that too.
- **Hard discipline:** we may say "X% of sampled NorCal firms could not competently intake the
  Spanish-language scenario" ONLY once X is measured. Until then the chapter is described as
  *forthcoming*, never quoted. No claimant data, ever, without consent (§VI). We test our own
  scripted Spanish scenario, not any real Spanish-speaking claimant's call.

> COMPLIANCE NOTE (§IV/§VI): The Spanish delta is the single most quotable — and most
> defamable-if-wrong — number in the report. It ships only with a confidence tier, a stated N,
> and Yang's protocol sign-off. A justice claim with a shaky number is worse than no claim.
