## 0. The governing decision: scorer -> closer is a SEQUENCE, not a binary

v1 recommended "stay scorer" and argued it purely on trust/believability. That was one-sided:
it hid the revenue ceiling. Corrected position:

**A scorer is a diagnosis; a closer is a cure, and buyers pay far more for cures.** The scorer
lives inside the *tool* budget ($2,500 Core sits next to $95-400 receptionist tools). The closer
reaches into the *case-acquisition* budget ($284/lead, $131.63 CPL, $9.87 CPC) — a wallet an
order of magnitude bigger. A pure-scorer strategy optimizes first-sale trust at the cost of ACV
and of durable recurring value (a diagnosis has a utility cliff; a cure earns its fee daily).

**But the closer is also the single hardest thing to sell this buyer, and the downside is
asymmetric enough to kill the company:** accuracy is his #1 concern (74.7%), Op. 512 makes him
personally liable for the vendor's AI, a PI firm (internal note: use only in strategy, never in
public copy) was sanctioned over its own AI, incumbents have pre-loaded "AI lacks empathy/
judgment," and research shows empathetic-AI overclaim *reduces* perceived authenticity. One
mishandled 2am call to an injured caller is a bar complaint.

**Resolution: sequence it.**
- **Scorer is the wedge.** Free audit, near-zero regulatory downside, and it is the most
  trust-matched message we could build (independence gets *stronger* the more skeptical the
  reader is; no competitor argues "nobody grades their own homework"). It earns the relationship.
- **The closer is the expansion act, sold only to firms the scorer already earned, on their own
  proven leak, as a supervised human-approved capability** — never the hero, never "AI closer/
  empathetic AI," always the Smith.ai inverse objection posture (concede limits, a human handles
  the sensitive moment). Reconcile its per-signed-case pricing conflict (§I) with Yang *before*
  any closing-flavored claim is drafted.

**Falsifiable trigger to revisit (v1 gave none):**
- *Move toward the closer* if beta firms take the free audit and the honest answer to "great,
  now what?" is "make your own callbacks," and audit->paid stalls at that gap. That means the
  scorer manufactures an emotion the product doesn't discharge.
- *Stay scorer-only longer* if firms convert on the diagnosis alone, or the first AI-mishandled
  call triggers real regulatory exposure.

Bridge copy for the expansion act (flat-fee-safe, sold to an already-trusting firm):
> "You've seen which callers walked. When you're ready, the same desk can make the first move to
> win them back. Every message drafted by us, approved by your staff, sent by no one until a
> human says go."

## 1. The buyer model, compressed (what every line must serve)

- **Unit of value is the signed case, not the lead.** Say "cases / signable cases / retainers."
  "Leads" and "vanity metrics" repel. (This rule also governs our own copy — see the "that lead"
  fix in §4.)
- **Fear order: accuracy -> confidentiality -> regulatory.** Earn trust in that priority.
- **Rule 7.1 brain: guarantees, superlatives, unverifiable numbers read as violations.**
  "Guaranteed" is radioactive. Understatement and admitted limits build trust. Short wick for BS.
- **Peer proof beats vendor claims** (73% trust peers, 3% trust vendor claims). No logos yet;
  the honest substitute is the audit itself + anonymized aggregate proof (§3.4b).
- **Identity: fighter / champion / David vs. Goliath, status measured in verdict dollars.**
- **Loss aversion in their own words:** "you paid for that call, then no one closed it,"
  "the next firm that answers gets the case," the leaky bucket (worn — demote the metaphor,
  promote the specific).

## 2. Competitive whitespace (what nobody else owns)

Saturated / table stakes (leading with any of these makes us invisible): "never miss a call,"
"24/7," "bilingual English/Spanish," "capture every lead." Several competitors share verbatim copy.

Open lanes, strongest first:
1. **Independence / "nobody grades their own homework."** Uncontested. The wedge.
2. **"What already walked, and what it cost" (post-answer forensics).** Everyone sells the *next*
   call; nobody tells you which *already-answered* callers you lost, reconciled against who
   actually signed.
3. **Correct citation as a trust signal** in a category that runs on fabricated stats.
4. **The adversary/asymmetry frame** (compliantly stated — see §3.5).
5. **Outcome reconciliation** — the least-hyped, most-defensible feature; it's the proof the
   number isn't vendor math. Raise it everywhere.

Trap: "autonomous closing / empathetic AI voice" looks like whitespace (only Supio/CaseGen touch
it) but lands on the buyer's biggest fear. Whitespace you shouldn't homestead as the lead.

## 3. The work, ordered by funnel leverage

### 3.1 The /audit uploader — HIGHEST LEVERAGE (v1 missed this entirely)

The moment a skeptical attorney uploads confidential recorded client calls is a live Rule 1.6 /
Op. 512 gut-check and the highest-abandonment step in the funnel. The reassurance exists but is
buried on /security. Put it **on the upload control itself**, welded to the independence claim:

> "Your recordings never train anyone's AI. Audio is deleted the second it's transcribed; the
> transcript and report are purged within 72 hours of your readout. NDA and BAA available before
> you send a single file."

And the independence-as-trust line right beside the CTA (the partner red-team's "one unlock"):
> "An outside read on your intake, from a desk paid the same whether it finds you nothing or a
> fortune."

This single pairing answers his #1 objection (why trust the number) and his #2 fear (is this a
Bar problem) in one breath, at the exact instant of maximum hesitation. Ship this before touching
the hero.

### 3.2 The letter's opening (the tip of the spear)

The letter gets the meeting; the homepage does not. A power pass should interrogate whether the
letter's *first paragraph* earns the next click, not just mine it for one line. Recommended focus
for the next letter revision (needs Ali sign-off + version bump): lead the opening with the
asymmetry-done-right frame (§3.5) and drive to the free audit as the falsifiable dare ("don't
argue with me, send me ten calls"). Keep the signed, staked-word voice; keep the CIPA/consent
carefulness. Do not compress the "thirty years" claim into a slogan (§3.5).

### 3.3 The free-audit report as a forwardable sales document (under-weighted in v1)

For a peer-proof-dependent, show-me buyer, **the deliverable is the persuasion.** The written
report the firm keeps is simultaneously the demonstration asset (real, on their own data,
unfakeable), the audit->paid converter, the thing forwarded to the partner who wasn't on the
call, and — anonymized and aggregated — the seed of a proprietary benchmark. Design it as a
forwarded sales document:
- A title page a managing partner will read cold (one-line finding, the estimated dollar range,
  the analyst signature).
- One plain-English finding per flagged call, each with its transcript moment (the "reason why"
  built in).
- One anonymizable, quote-worthy summary line the firm could — with consent — let us reuse.

This is the highest-ROI *asset* to design, distinct from any single line of site copy.

### 3.4 Outreach subject line / first sentence

More leveraged than the H1 and currently unaddressed. The buyer model ("79% hire the first to
respond helpfully; short wick for BS") says the subject line decides whether the letter is read.
Draft candidates (conviction picks, not A/B — see §6):
- "The one intake number your firm has never measured"
- "An outside read on where your intake leaks, free, on ten of your own calls"
Keep it firm-directed, non-blast, opt-out present (§III).

### 3.5 The homepage hero (correctly ranked ~5th, still worth fixing)

**Ship Hero A now** (fixes the live em-dash violation in your own most-read line):
> "See the signable cases that walked out of your intake, and what they cost you."

**Kill Hero B as written.** Both the partner and the compliance reviewer independently rejected
*"The other side has scored these calls for thirty years. Now you can too."* Three defects: (1)
false antecedent — the insurance side scores the *claimant's statement to the adjuster* (Colossus-
style software), not your privileged pre-case intake calls, so a trial lawyer's first reaction is
"no they haven't, what are you talking about"; (2) "thirty years" is an uncited hard number
headlining the site (fails §IV/§V); (3) "scored these calls" in isolation flirts with a
surveillance read (§II optics).

**Compliant rewrite that keeps the David-vs-Goliath punch** (converged recommendation of both
red-teams):
> "The insurance side has scored injury claims with software for decades. Almost no one scores
> the intake call that starts the case. Now you can."

Subhead (add "estimated," fix the certainty overclaim per §IV):
> "We read your firm's recorded intake calls, flag the callers our analysis marks as signable who
> never signed, and put an estimated dollar figure on the misses. Independently, for a flat fee,
> never a share of anything. Start with a free audit of 10 calls."

"Independently, for a flat fee, never a share of anything" is the strongest single trust line and
belongs in the hero. Weld flat-fee to independence every time it appears — the flat fee is the
*proof* of the independence, not a footnote ("we get paid the same whether we find you $10k or
$500k").

### 3.6 NEW ASSET — the independence comparison table

`INDEPENDENCE_LINE` is begging to be a grid; a math buyer wants the visual, not the paragraph.

| | AI receptionist | Your agency | Your staff | **Intake QA** |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Grades its own work? | Yes | Yes | Yes | **No** |
| Scores calls it didn't answer? | No | No | No | **Yes** |
| Reconciles against who actually signed? | No | No | No | **Yes** |
| Paid the same no matter what it finds? | No | No | No | **Yes** |

Only the last column earns four. This is the most on-brand persuasion object for the independence
wedge. State it precisely; no disparagement (§V). Build it.

### 3.7 NEW ASSET — the named four-failure-mode "reason why"

"Good cases slip, that's math" is too soft for a math brain. Name the failure modes; it makes the
product feel actuarial (Meehl/Grove — Ali's own architecture) instead of vibes:
> "A signable case walks for one of four reasons: the caller hit voicemail, the rep never asked
> the injury question, the callback came a day late, or a Spanish speaker hit an English wall.
> We tell you which one, per case."

### 3.8 Elevate the founder origin story (currently one buried constant)

If a human signs every flag, that human is the brand. `WHO_DOES_THE_WORK` should be a presence,
not a line:
> "I was the paralegal who watched signable cases die on hold, and no one would grade it. So I
> built the grader."
Borrowed credibility + David-vs-Goliath in one sentence. The partner red-team: "one founder is
thinner than 'independent desk' makes it sound" — so show Ali, ideally scoring a sample call.

### 3.9 Fix the product's weakest link — "why does month six exist?"

The partner's sharpest hit: the subscription argues for its own cancellation (the leak shrinks,
so ROI shrinks, so month six looks like recurring bad news). The retention story in
`MONTH_6_ITEMS` is half-labeled "in development" (vaporware carrying the retention narrative).
Reframe the recurring value as a **standing independent instrument you'd feel naked without**, not
"we'll keep finding leaks":
> "After the first audit, the desk becomes the one number on your intake that isn't self-graded,
> every month. The independent scorecard your intake manager is measured against, the way you'd
> never drop your malpractice carrier."
Sell the standing instrument; stop marketing the in-development items as if live.

## 4. Verified-stat ledger + the required fixes

**Safe to cite (attributed, non-truncated):** 79% hire the first firm that responds *helpfully*
(Clio) — the word "helpfully" is NON-TRUNCATABLE (drop it and it becomes the quarantined folk-
stat); only 40% of firms answer the phone / 48% essentially unreachable / 73% wouldn't recommend
(Clio 2024, 500-firm mystery shop — verify 40% and 79% are the *same* report before joining them);
5-minute rule 21x qualify / ~100x connect (MIT/Oldroyd, NOT Harvard); PI = highest-CPC vertical
$9.87 / highest CPL $131.63 (WordStream 2026); ABA Op. 512; US Hispanic population 63.7M / 19%
(Census).

**Required fixes (from the compliance red-team):**
- **Reconcile the two lead-cost figures.** The doc cannot bless $131.63 CPL (WordStream) and
  headline $284/lead (Pareto) — ~2x apart, same measure. Pick the one whose method you can show
  and state the definition, or drop the dollar CPL and keep the qualitative "<10% of firms know
  their own cost." Do not publish two contradictory CPLs.
- **Kill "Gone for good."** An absolute your own rescue product disproves (self-defeating + 7.1
  overclaim). Use: "Miss the callback and the next firm that answers gets the case."
- **Morgan & Morgan sanction: internal strategy only, never public copy.** In public, cite the
  phenomenon + ABA Op. 512 ("courts have already sanctioned lawyers for AI hallucinations")
  without naming a competitor firm (false-light risk + reads badly to an ethics-minded buyer).
- **"that lead" violates our own "say cases, not leads" rule.** Rewrite loss frames to "You
  already paid to make that phone ring, and no one closed it."
- **Attribute WEBRIS (<10%) to primary or label "as reported by WEBRIS";** don't lead the
  credibility bar with a secondary agency stat.
- **48.4% LA-Hispanic:** attribute (Census/ACS, year, LA County), keep as demographic context
  only, never adjacent to a conversion implication.

**Do NOT publish (research-flagged unverifiable):** every Spanish/bilingual conversion percentage;
"80% never reconnect"; the bare "78% hire the first firm to respond" folk-stat. Keep the
quarantined "400% / 5-minute" multiplier dead.

## 5. Voice rules (consolidated, enforce on every line)

- No em dashes. Global sweep to hyphens/periods before the next outreach wave (the live H1
  violates this today).
- Say "cases / retainers," never "leads."
- No "guaranteed," no superlatives ("only/best/#1"), no hype adjectives ("revolutionary,"
  "game-changing," "seamless," "leverage," "supercharge"), no "AI-powered" in any headline.
- Estimates carry stated confidence; never a probabilistic inference as certainty (§IV).
- Concrete numbers and named mechanisms over adjectives. Admit limits; it builds trust.
- Every claim carries a citation or is cut (§IV).
