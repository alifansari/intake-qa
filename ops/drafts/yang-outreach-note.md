## Why this version (what the research changed)

- **Honest, specific subject line** — the old "a quick question in your wheelhouse" was a small dishonesty (this is real paid work, not quick), and she clocks that instantly. Worst possible first impression *for her*.
- **Cut "I'd rather have it early than explain myself later"** — to an ethics prosecutor's ear that reads as consciousness of a problem being pre-managed. It was the single most damaging line.
- **Lead with the ask, not flattery** — vague warmth ("chewing on a few things") is the boilerplate tell senior people are tired of. Name the *one* real thing she said (you fill it in) or cut it.
- **No statute parade** — the earlier draft lumped **AB 931 in with the advertising rules, which is wrong** (AB 931 is a fee-sharing/ABS statute, not advertising). A miscited cluster is exactly the tell she'd downgrade you for. The email now carries no chapter-and-verse; the memo does the legal work. **Bonus you should know:** AB 931 has a *flat-fee exemption* that likely **blesses** your model — so it's a point in your favor, not a threat (the memo should foreground that; I've flagged it).
- **Defer on money and process** — dropped "small paid review" (that pre-sizes her fee). You pay her *usual rate* on *her* terms; senior attorneys scope and paper the engagement themselves.
- **First ask = permission to send the docs**, not a call. A 20-minute call to review two legal memos under-scopes the very work you're paying for (and reads as not understanding it). The call comes after she's seen scope.
- **Defused the father dynamic** — she may feel a soft, resented obligation because you're her advisor's son. Naming and releasing it turns the landmine into a credibility signal.
- **Foregrounded "records nothing"** — a no-recording mystery shop in a two-party-consent state *shows* your character instead of asserting "doing it right." It's your strongest signal.
- **"Best," not "Warmly"** — "Warmly" claims a closeness not yet earned with someone you've met once.
