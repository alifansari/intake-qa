## 1. The statutory landscape as of July 2026 (verified status)

### 1.1 AB 931 (Kalra) — State Bar Act; consumer legal funding; fee-sharing & "anything of value"
- **[VERIFIED] Enacted.** Approved by the Governor and filed with the Secretary of State **October 10, 2025**. Chaptered, 2025–2026 Regular Session.
  - Primary: leginfo — https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB931
  - Chaptered text: https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB931/id/3272882
- **[VERIFIED] Two distinct operative prohibitions are bundled in AB 931:**
  1. **An ABS/out-of-state fee-sharing ban** — bars a CA attorney from sharing legal fees, directly or indirectly, with an out-of-state entity that provides legal services while permitting nonlawyer ownership or decision-making authority (an "Alternative Business Structure"). Applies to **contracts entered into on or after January 1, 2026**; **sunsets January 1, 2030**. (Bloomberg Law; Holland & Knight.)
  2. **An "anything of value" prohibition** — an attorney "shall not compensate, promise, or give anything of value to a person for the purpose of recommending or securing the services of the attorney or the attorney's law firm," subject to enumerated exceptions (e.g., permitted gifts, bar-operated lawyer referral services). (JDSupra/Shipkevich summary, prior insight 2026-07-07 A1; confirmed in this pass via CalMatters and secondary summaries.)
  - Secondary (verified against each other): Bloomberg Law "California Bans Contingent Fee Sharing With 'Alternative' Firms" — https://news.bloomberglaw.com/business-and-practice/california-bans-contingent-fee-sharing-with-alternative-firms ; Holland & Knight "Regulatory Retrenchment in California: What AB 931 Means…" — https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/regulatory-retrenchment-in-california-what-ab-931-means ; CalMatters Digital Democracy — https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202520260ab931
- **[VERIFIED — money quote, multi-source] A flat-fee carve-out exists.** AB 931 was amended to **exempt "fixed fee arrangements, including those used for back office and other services"** and **"flat fee sharing arrangements for specific dollar amounts"** — arrangements that **do not pay for referrals or lead generation and do not scale payment based on the amount recovered** (Bloomberg Law; corroborated by the leginfo bill analysis and the first-pass search summary).

### 1.2 SB 37 (Umberg) — Attorneys: unlawful solicitations and advertisements
- **[VERIFIED] Enacted.** **Chapter 645, Statutes of 2025**; effective **January 1, 2026**.
  - Primary: leginfo — https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB37

- **[VERIFIED] What it does:** (a) toughens the **runner/capper** prohibitions (soliciting business for attorneys in/around prisons, jails, hospitals, public places, private property); (b) tightens **attorney-advertising** content rules — bars misleading/deceptive/false statements about a lawyer's skills, experience, or record, and bars references to recognitions/awards conferred merely for membership or paid-for; (c) creates a **private right of action** — "any person" may sue for statutory damages, attorney's fees, injunctive relief, and other relief; the court sets statutory damages considering the seriousness of the misconduct and the defendant's net worth.
- **[VERIFIED — as reflected in our own live copy] Damages band cited on `/compliance`:** statutory damages **$5,000 minimum to $100,000 maximum per violation, or 3× actual damages, whichever is larger**, plus fees. - **[VERIFIED] SB 37's broadened "advertisement" definition is claimant-facing.** It targets communications intended to encourage *individuals* to secure a lawyer's services — i.e., potential clients, not law-firm buyers. ## 2. Our structure, then prohibition-by-prohibition analysis

### 2.1 The facts of what Intake QA is (the operative facts your analysis rests on)
1. **Fee:** a **flat monthly subscription** (tiered by call volume), invariant to outcomes — the same whether the firm signs zero cases or fifty. Never a percentage of recovery, never per-case, per-signed-case, per-settlement, or per-recovered-dollar. (compliance-invariants §I; enforced in code by a structural flat-fee hard-fail in the packaging config.)
2. **What we do:** ingest the firm's **own** recorded intake calls (their consent chain), score them against a fixed rubric, and surface leaked signable cases so the **firm's own staff** call back their **own** prior inbound callers.
3. **Who we contact:** **no one.** The tool never contacts a stranger and never sends autonomously; a person at the firm approves every message (Rule 5.3 posture). We never dial claimants for benchmarking (CIPA-safe mystery-shop only, Yang-signed, no live claimants).
4. **Direction of value:** we are paid **by the firm, for analysis of the firm's own funnel.** We never receive value from, or route claimants to, any lawyer for a fee. We are a QA/software vendor, positioned as the **independent scorer** — Ali is "Analyst of Record," **not a fee participant.**

### 2.2 AB 931 — "anything of value for recommending or securing services"
- **Trigger:** an attorney compensating/promising/giving anything of value **for the purpose of recommending or securing** the attorney's services.
- **Our analysis [OUR CONCLUSION — needs Yang]:** The prohibition targets **payment aimed at recommending/securing** a lawyer's services — the runner/lead-gen vector. **Intake QA is the opposite vector:** we do not recommend, secure, refer, or procure any claimant for any lawyer. We score the firm's **own** inbound calls and help the firm re-contact its **own** callers. The firm pays us for **analysis of its own funnel**, not for bringing it business. The trigger ("for the purpose of recommending or securing services") is therefore never pulled. The flat-fee, recovery-decoupled structure independently keeps us out of anything that could be read as outcome-based procurement.
- **Corroboration (not load-bearing):** AB 931's own **flat-fee carve-out** ("fixed fee arrangements, including those used for back office and other services"; "flat fee sharing arrangements for specific dollar amounts"; no referral/lead-gen pay; no scaling on recovery) describes our fee almost verbatim — **but** see the §1.1 caveat: that carve-out appears to sit in the ABS-fee-sharing prong, so we present it as corroboration, not as a statutory safe harbor for the recommending/securing prong.

### 2.3 AB 931 — ABS / out-of-state fee-sharing ban
- **Trigger:** sharing legal fees with an out-of-state, nonlawyer-owned entity providing legal services.
- **Our analysis [OUR CONCLUSION]:** Facially inapplicable. Intake QA is **not an ABS**, does **not provide legal services**, and **shares no legal fees** — there is nothing to split because our fee is a flat subscription, not a portion of any recovery. This prong is about law-firm ownership/structure; we are a vendor to the firm.

### 2.4 SB 37 / B&P §§6151–6152 (runner-capper) + private right of action
- **Trigger:** acting, **for consideration**, as an agent for a lawyer in the **solicitation or procurement of business** (§6151), which §6152 prohibits and §6153 criminalizes; SB 37 adds a consumer private right of action and toughens the regime.
- **Our analysis [OUR CONCLUSION — this is the load-bearing one]:** Two independent reasons we sit outside it: **(1) Compensation is not tied to procuring or recovering any case.** A flat fee that does not rise or fall with signings or recoveries cannot fairly be characterized as payment to an agent for soliciting/procuring clients. **(2) We never solicit or procure new business.** We help the firm re-contact **its own** prior inbound callers — quality control and internal follow-up, not an agent bringing strangers. §6154 voids contracts procured through a runner/capper; we procure no contracts. SB 37's private right of action raises the **stakes** of getting this wrong (which is *why* the flat-fee bright line is non-negotiable), but does not change the analysis.

### 2.5 Rule 5.4 (fee-sharing with nonlawyers)
- **Our analysis [OUR CONCLUSION]:** Not the governing lead authority for a CA firm, and not triggered regardless. Our fee is a **flat monthly subscription, not a share of any legal fee** — there is nothing to divide. Rule 5.4's concern (a nonlawyer taking a cut of legal fees and thereby influencing professional judgment) is structurally absent. For out-of-CA readers who reach for ABA Model Rule 5.4, the same "nothing to split" answer holds; for CA firms the runner/capper framework governs. (This mirrors the current `/compliance` footnote — see §3, item F.)

### 2.6 Rule 7.2(b) (paying a nonlawyer for recommending the lawyer's services)
- **Our analysis [OUR CONCLUSION]:** Not triggered. **We are not paid to recommend the firm to anyone and we do not refer.** 7.2(b)'s carve-outs (reasonable advertising costs, qualified referral-service charges, Rule 1.17 practice purchase, reciprocal-referral arrangements) are not even reached, because the base prohibition — payment **for recommending** — does not describe a QA/software fee. Note 7.2(b)(1)'s "reasonable costs" gloss confirms the drafters' worry is **disguised fee-sharing dressed as advertising**; a flat QA subscription is neither advertising nor a recommendation.

### 2.7 Rule 7.3 (solicitation)
- **Our analysis [OUR CONCLUSION — pending the 7.3(f)/Comment [5] confirmation in §1.4]:** Intake QA solicits **law firms (businesses), not claimants** — the distinction compliance-invariants §III requires us to keep crisp. Within the firm's use of the tool, save-protocol communications go **only to people who already called the firm** (a response to the prospective client's own inquiry), carry an opt-out, and require human approval. We do **not** claim the save protocol is categorically outside 7.3; we give the firm the controls and its counsel makes the call. **Yang: does the June 1, 2026 7.3(f)/Comment [5] amendment disturb any of this?**
