## LACBA listservs: closed, and the rules are published

LACBA's Listserve Guidelines are public (lacba.org/?pg=Listserve-Guidelines) [verified]. Operative language: the listserv program is "a members-only benefit, intended exclusively for official business of the group and is not to be used for personal, political, and/or commercial purposes... or to advertise or market non-LACBA related programs or events." Both the **Litigation Section** and a **Small Firm Section** exist [verified], each with listservs — but a vendor methodology article carrying Ali's name/company is commercial content under this rule, even with the $375 Associate membership. Violations are reported to the Section's moderator; peer bars (Iowa, King County, Philadelphia) publish removal-for-commercial-posts policies [verified], though no publicly documented LACBA vendor-removal incident was found [verified absence, not absence of enforcement].

**The get-a-member-to-share-it question: it's a policy dodge — don't orchestrate it.** A member posting the branded piece at our request is proxy commercial use of a channel that bans commercial use; if traced back, it burns the member and brands us as the vendor who gamed the bar's listserv. The compliant version: publish on owned channels; if an attorney client genuinely finds it useful and shares it unprompted, that is their speech. We may tell clients it exists; we may not script or request the listserv post.

**What's genuinely unpublished, and whom to ask:** (1) whether Associate ($375) members may join sections and receive listservs at all — ask LACBA Member Services, msd@lacba.org / (213) 896-6560: "As a non-attorney Associate member, may I join the Litigation and Small Firm Sections and subscribe to their listservs, and what posting privileges apply?" (2) Litigation Section vendor-sponsorship rates — the lacba.org sponsorship inquiry form.
