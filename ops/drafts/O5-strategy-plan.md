## 1. The one-paragraph thesis

Intake QA is a **$1M business that is not gated by market or price, and a defensible business whose
defensibility is not yet earned.** The path to $1M ARR is ~26-31 paying firms (~28 blended under the
settled Table C) — roughly **2-3% of the serviceable CA solo/small-PI niche.** That is a founder-led
selling problem, fully bootstrappable, not a capital problem. What makes it *defensible* is a single
structural fact the funded incumbents cannot copy without dismantling the products that fund them:
**we are the independent scorer with no downstream product to flatter — the Moody's / J.D. Power
position.** But that fact is only a *sentence* until it is welded to an authority stack (published
benchmark + false-alarm rate + signed attestation + named standard), and the window to own the word
"independent" before the market's vocabulary hardens is **~6-12 months, not the 24-36 the structural
argument implies.** The binding constraint on *both* the revenue ramp and the moat is the same scarce
resource: **the attestation-bound hours of a named Analyst of Record.** The property that makes the
moat uncopyable (a person personally stakes their credibility on each signed statement — Austin's
speech-act) is the exact property that caps a solo desk below the firm count $1M requires. **$1M is
therefore structurally a ≥2-Analyst-of-Record number, reached by bootstrapping, on a clock set by an
independence land-grab that is running right now.**

## 2. What makes this a $1M-in-a-year business

**It is not TAM.** Serviceable CA solo/small PI universe ≈ 800-1,500 firms (insight 2026-07-07 C1,
ESTIMATE); $1M needs ~28 of them = 2-3% penetration. The market has ~50× the headroom the goal needs.

**It is not price.** Table C is settled and flat (`pricing-final-onepager.md`, 2026-07-12): Core
$2,500 / Pro $5,000 / Founding $1,500×12mo. The unit-economics refresh confirms the firm count barely
moved (24 → ~26-31) and nothing crosses the §I bright line.

**It is conversion velocity × the analyst-hour ceiling.** Both surviving constraints are downstream of
the funnel, and both are *knowable and improvable from firm #1*:
- **The timeline dial — pilot→paid conversion.** The old 50% base case is the *median for a
  sales-assisted, structured, high-commitment pilot in our $15-35K-ACV band* — defensible only if the
  pilot behaves that way. **Our beta is FREE and open-ended**, which benchmarks 8-25% (opt-in band) and
  selects for tire-kickers. Realized pilot→paid is plausibly **25-40%, not 50%**, and at 33% the $1M
  *exit run-rate* slips from ~12 months to **~18** (at 25%, ~24). The fix is not a price cut — it is a
  **commitment device on the free pilot** (written success criteria + a pre-agreed conversion date);
  structured pilots convert ~3.2× open-ended ones, so the *same free audit* yields 1.5-3× the closes.
- **The ceiling dial — attestation-bound analyst hours** (see §4). This one caps how many firms one
  human can *hold* at defensible quality, independent of how fast they convert.

**Honesty flag carried forward:** $1M is an **exit run-rate in ~month 12** even in the good case, not
year-1 booked revenue. Average recognized ARR across year 1 is far below $1M. Say that out loud to any
founder/investor framing (insight B3; unit-econ §2b).

## 3. What makes it defensible — and why the moat is earned, not owned

**The feature wedge is dead.** "We score intake calls" is now table stakes: Supio (Scoring Agent grades
every call and human rep against the firm's SOP), Eve/EveOS (Jenny scores + signs on the call; Auditor
reviews docs nightly), Perspective, and EvenUp-adjacent all do it in 2026 (competitive-moat §0-1,
verified). Any deck line implying we are unusual for scoring calls is refutable the moment a prospect
Googles the category, and violates §V. **Durability: 0 months. Bench it for good.**

**The structural wedge is intact and, against the incumbents, uncopyable.** Independence + no voice
agent + no demand product + no case-management labor + no CRM = **nothing downstream the score could
flatter.** For Supio, Eve, EvenUp, or Perspective to *become* independent they would have to stop
selling the thing that pays their bills. That is the incumbent's dilemma made structural, and it is the
whole business: a rating agency that also underwrote the bonds it rated would not be a rating agency.
**Durable 24-36+ months while they stay bundled.**

**But the moat is a *positioning* moat, and its salience is perishable.** Most 2-10-attorney PI firms
do not yet know to *ask* for an independent scorer, because the category has only ever been sold to them
as a feature of a tool they already use. Independence has commercial value only once (i) the firm
understands its intake tool is marking its own homework, and (ii) an *authority* exists that makes
"independently verified" a legible, askable-for thing. **We have not built that authority yet.** Whoever
builds it first owns the definition of independence the way Moody's owns "investment grade." So the
practical land-grab window is **~6-12 months** — bounded not by the incumbents (safe for years) but by
(a) a purpose-built *independent* entrant claiming the slot with more capital than a pre-revenue solo
can match (tripwire #8, currently unseen — the cheapest thing to be blindsided by), or (b) the slot
simply never becoming salient because no one taught the market to value it.

**The defensibility is a four-layer stack, not a single claim** (competitive-moat §4). Each layer is
something a bundled vendor structurally will not build, and each compounds *more* valuable as the field
crowds with self-grading scorers:
1. **Structural independence** (the lock — free to hold, impossible to copy, but on its own just a sentence).
2. **Published methodology + false-alarm rate** (legibility — the ROC/signal-detection move; turns a vibe into an instrument; B-006 benchmark + Calibration page do double duty).
3. **The signed, staked attestation** (the un-SaaS-able layer — a dashboard cannot sign its name; the difference between a credit *tool* and a credit *rating*).
4. **The develop-queue / SLA conveyor** (the operational value that survives feature commoditization — when "we score the call" is worthless because everyone does it, "every unresolved value-determining question is worked to resolution on a clock, here's the ledger" is what the firm pays to keep; B-016/017).

**The uncomfortable corollary (Ali's honesty rule):** if we do *not* build layers 2-4 fast, layer 1 is
not a moat and we are a feature company competing on price against firms that raised $150M (EvenUp,
now $2B+) and $103M (Eve) this year. The crowding that killed the feature wedge is the same crowding
that makes independence salient — but only for whoever is the legible, credentialed, already-published
independent when the need arrives.

## 4. Where the binding constraint really is: attestation-bound analyst hours

Both quantitative drafts land in the same place from different directions: **the wall is one Analyst of
Record's reviewable hours, and the moat *is* the wall.** The signed attestation (§IV; Austin
speech-act) cannot be produced by a junior or emitted by a dashboard, so the property that makes Intake
QA uncopyable is the property that makes it un-scalable past one analyst's review capacity.

**Two loads compete for the same finite budget:**
- **Acquisition** (one-time per close): sourcing + outreach + the free Leak Audit (~4-6 hrs — the AoR
  must personally hear every call to stake the signature; you cannot cite what you did not hear) +
  onboarding ≈ ~35 analyst-hours per net close at 50% pilot→paid, rising to ~51 at 33%.
- **Delivery** (recurring monthly, forever): review flagged subset + QC + sign the monthly
  Missed-Revenue Statement ≈ ~3-6 hrs/firm/month, growing monotonically with every firm closed and
  pushed toward the top of the band by Pro tier, high call volume, and Spanish (double-language) review.

**Reconciling the two drafts' ceiling numbers (honest flag — they used different hour budgets):**

| Draft | Assumed review budget | Solo delivery ceiling | Recommended Analyst-#2 trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| capital-strategy §3 | ~15-20 hrs/wk to *review* | ~13-16 firms | ~firm 12 (~month 8-9) |
| unit-economics §3 | ~120 desk-hrs/mo total, ~4 hrs/firm | ~20 while acquiring / ~30 serve-only | ~firm 15-18 |

These are the *same model* at two points on the hours-per-firm × available-hours surface; the gap is an
assumption gap, not a contradiction. **The load-bearing conclusion is identical and survives either
input: the solo ceiling (~13-20 firms while still acquiring) sits BELOW the ~26-31 firms $1M requires.**
$1M is not reachable by a single Analyst of Record at defensible attestation quality. **Take the
conservative edge and pre-commit the Analyst-#2 trigger at ~firm 12-15 (~month 8-9), not "later."** The
break-mode arrives exactly when you are trying to accelerate (delivery hours have eaten the week right as
firms 15→28 need acquisition hours), so it must be planned *before* it bites.

**The relief valves (all moat-compatible):**
- **Automate the wedge, never the signature.** Push the free autopsy/Readout toward a machine-generated
  *marketing* diagnostic (resolve B-015 so it is framed as the visitor's own artifact); keep the paid
  monthly statement's named-human attestation sacrosanct. A PDF a machine emitted is a *report*; a PDF a
  named analyst signed is a *rating*. Lifts the acquisition ceiling at **zero moat cost** if the line stays bright.
- **Hire a second NAMED Analyst of Record**, self-funded from cash flow (~$80-120k/yr ≈ covered by ~4-6
  Pro or ~8-12 Core firms; affordability point ~firm 8-12 nearly coincides with the breakpoint ~firm
  12-15 — the business pays for its own ceiling-lift). J.D. Power / Moody's are institutions of *many*
  named analysts; multiple independently-staking analysts is the *mature* form of the model, not a
  dilution. **Two rules make it moat-positive not moat-diluting:** (a) comp must be *independent of
  whether a firm likes its score* — a salesperson-who-signs re-creates the rating-agency no-Chinese-wall
  failure; (b) run a periodic double-scored calibration sample and publish the inter-rater agreement rate
  *before* the hire, so the second signature strengthens the methodology story instead of drifting it.
- **Treat review-time-per-call as a unit-economics feature.** The conveyor's surfaced citations (B-016)
  shave minutes off the ~15-min/call review; every minute directly raises the ceiling. Prioritize it as
  economics, not just UX.

## 5. The three highest-leverage strategic bets

**Bet 1 — Win the independence land-grab in the next 6-12 months (the clock that dominates everything
else).** Re-weight the authority stack from "months-to-pay-off nice-to-have" back toward the critical
path, because it is now *also* competitive defense: earmark the beta's consented autopsy data to seed
the CIPA-safe benchmark (B-006 → B-001), and ship a minimal **published methodology + false-alarm-rate**
page **with the first paying firm, not later**, alongside the signed attestation and a named standard.
Add tripwire #8 (an independent/neutral-scorer entrant) as the top-watched signal. This is the bet that
converts a perishable sentence into an owned position before someone with $150M does. *Highest leverage
because it is the only bet with a hard external deadline set by an actor we don't control.*

**Bet 2 — Break the analyst-hour ceiling on purpose, on two fronts at once.** (a) Put a **commitment
device on the free pilot** (written success criteria + pre-agreed conversion date) to pull realized
pilot→paid from ~30% toward the ~50% the timeline needs — the single cheapest way to keep the ramp near
12 months instead of 18-24. (b) **Automate the wedge / never the signature** to lift the acquisition
ceiling for free, and **pre-commit the Analyst-#2 hire at firm ~12-15**, funded from cash flow, with the
calibration protocol written first. *Highest leverage because it attacks the one constraint that gates
both revenue velocity and the moat, and because the fix self-funds at almost exactly the firm count where
the ceiling appears.*

**Bet 3 — Bootstrap, and treat independence as a capital-structure decision, not just positioning.**
Do not open a venture raise: its incentive vector is *anti-correlated with the moat.* A growth-mandated
fiduciary's return-maximizing moves point straight at (i) outcome-tied / per-signed / usage pricing — a
**§I bright-line violation and an SB 37 private-right-of-action exposure ($5,000-$100,000/violation),
not merely off-brand** — and (ii) becoming a vendor *inside* the firm's stack, which re-creates the
issuer-pays conflict that destroyed rating-agency credibility in 2008 (no Chinese wall between the rater
and the revenue). We need 24-ish firms, not 24,000 — we do not have the distribution problem venture
money solves. Keep exactly one narrow instrument on the shelf, unused: **RBF (Founderpath-class,
~1.1-1.5×, no board seat, no personal guarantee)** to pull the Analyst-#2 hire forward *only if*
delivery proves the bottleneck before cash flow funds it. If any outside dollar is ever seriously
considered, the **Independence Covenant** (flat-fee-forever, no vendor-capture, permanent false-alarm-
rate + signed attestation, no controlling board) goes to Yang for legal form first. *Highest leverage as
a negative bet: the single decision that most reliably destroys the moat is the one that feels most like
progress, so pre-committing against it is worth more than any growth tactic.*

## 6. The single biggest existential risk

**The two clocks collide, and the collision is invisible until it is fatal.** The moat's salience is
perishable (~6-12 months to own "independent" before a funded entrant claims it or the market's
vocabulary hardens around "scoring = a feature of my stack"), while the moat *is* the throughput
bottleneck (attestation caps a solo desk at ~13-20 firms, below $1M's ~28) — and the free open-ended
beta plausibly converts at ~30% not ~50%, stretching the ramp to ~18-24 months. **These compound in one
direction: the land-grab demands you accumulate published authority and paying-firm proof *fast*, but the
attestation ceiling plus below-benchmark free-pilot conversion physically throttle how fast you can, and
you will not see the shortfall in the data until several audits have already run.** The failure mode is
not going bankrupt — it is arriving at month 12-18 *structurally unique but commercially worthless*: the
independent slot has been defined by a better-capitalized player (or never became salient), and Intake QA
is a feature company competing on price against firms that raised $150M and $103M this year. **The moat is
real, but it is earned on a deadline, and the same scarce resource that earns it (the named analyst's
staked hours) is the resource the deadline outruns.** The entire O5 program is a bet that Bets 1-3 —
authority-first sequencing, a pre-committed second Analyst of Record, and refusing the capital that would
trade the moat for speed — let the earning outrun the perishing. If the benchmark + LACBA authority
assets fail to earn first meetings by ~month 4, this risk is materializing and the plan must be revisited
(the only defensible flip is a *covenant-bound* raise to accelerate **authority-building**, never
outcome-pricing).
