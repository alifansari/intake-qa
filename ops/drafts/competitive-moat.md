Four material moves in ~9 weeks. Three of them compress our window; one widens the moat.

1. **Supio went down-market in CONTENT — tripwire #4 FIRED.** The battlecard asserted "No
   published small-firm ICP [verified: absence on site]." **That is now false.** supio.com/customers
   now features a **solo** case study (Welsh & Welsh, "Like Having Three Lawyers"), a **rural
   small firm** (Prince Law Firm), and an explicit **3-attorney firm** study framed as "How a
   3-attorney firm levels the playing field against larger defense firms" [verified:
   supio.com/customers, fetched 2026-07-12]. Supio is now publishing proof aimed squarely at
   our beachhead segment. This is the single most important delta.

2. **Supio built a small-firm SALES motion — tripwire #3 partially FIRED.** A **"Partnership &
   Alliances Sales Executive"** role is "focused on driving revenue growth through the sale of
   **partnership products to small law firms**" [verified-as-reported: ZipRecruiter/Gem job
   listings surfaced 2026-07-12; exact live title not re-confirmed on supio.com/careers, which
   renders roles via an external Gem portal — treat as plausible-verified, re-check at source].
   Note the nuance: this is a *partnership/alliances* motion (selling small firms via channel),
   not yet a direct SMB inside-sales team. But it is the first small-firm go-to-market signal.

3. **EvenUp escalated massively — the bundle thesis is now over-proven.** EvenUp raised a
   **$150M Series E at a $2B+ valuation** (roughly doubled) [verified: Crunchbase News; evenuplaw.com]
   and launched **PLAAS (Pre-Litigation-as-a-Service)** — AI plus **EvenUp's own U.S.-based human
   case managers** running the full pre-lit lifecycle (claim setup, records retrieval, demand
   prep, negotiations, optional lien resolution), **>$10M in early subscriptions** [verified:
   businesswire/lawnext, May 2026]. EvenUp does NOT yet do call-content intake QA — its "march to
   intake" is a pre-lit *funnel/labor* play, not a call-scoring play. But PLAAS is the cleanest
   possible illustration of our Switzerland argument: a vendor that literally gets paid on the
   downstream case work cannot be the independent grader of whether that case should have been
   taken. Their model *is* the conflict.

4. **Eve shipped EveOS — the closest structural neighbor, still bundled.** EveOS ("the AI
   operating system for plaintiff law," June 2026) bundles **Eve Intake ("Jenny")** — autonomous
   voice agent that "answers calls, qualifies leads, scores cases, sends the retainer… on the
   same call," configurable against "case type, injury threshold, liability factors" — plus **Eve
   Auditor**, a *nightly* review of stored **case documents / medical records** surfacing missed
   injuries, treatment gaps, missing docs [verified: eve.legal/intake, /use-cases/ai-auditor,
   lawnext EveOS piece]. Eve is the only competitor that scores calls AND runs an "auditor," but
   (a) the Auditor reviews DOCUMENTS in signed matters, not intake CALLS, and (b) Jenny REPLACES
   the human rep rather than QAing the firm's own people. The full teardown lives in
   `competitive-eve-defense.md`; the incumbent's-dilemma moat there still holds.

**Net:** feature parity on "score the call" is complete across the funded field. The access/price
advantage of our small-firm beachhead is eroding (Supio content + partner motion). The *only*
thing that has not moved — and structurally cannot move without a competitor dismantling its own
model — is independence.

## 3. HONEST WEDGE-DURABILITY WINDOW

Split the wedge into its two halves and price them separately, because they expire on different
clocks.

**A) The FEATURE wedge ("we score intake calls") — EXPIRED.** Do not build GTM on it. Supio,
Eve, Perspective all score calls against firm-configured criteria in 2026 [verified]. Any deck
line implying we are unusual for scoring calls is refutable and violates §V the moment a prospect
Googles "AI intake scoring." Already flagged in insight 2026-07-10; now fully confirmed across
four named players. **Durability: 0 months. It's gone.**

**B) The STRUCTURAL wedge (independence + no downstream product + published methodology + signed
attestation) — durable against incumbents, fragile in salience.** Two clocks:

- **Against the bundled incumbents: 24–36+ months, effectively indefinite while they stay
  bundled.** For Supio, Eve, EvenUp, or Perspective to *become* independent they would have to
  stop selling the thing that pays their bills — the voice agent (Supio/Eve/Perspective) or the
  downstream case work (EvenUp PLAAS, Supio demands, Eve Auditor value-mining). This is the
  incumbent's dilemma made structural: they can copy the *feature* in one release (the Eve-defense
  doc is right that it's ~one Auditor release away technically), but they cannot copy the
  *position* without cannibalizing the product that funds them. That is the Moody's / J.D. Power /
  Consumer Reports lock — the buyer trusts the rating *because* the rater sells nothing else the
  rating could flatter. A rating agency that also underwrote the bonds it rated would not be a
  rating agency. **This half of the wedge is genuinely defensible and it is the whole business.**

- **The SALIENCE clock: ~6–12 months, and it is the binding constraint.** Independence is worth
  exactly what the market will pay for it — and today most 2–10-attorney PI firms do not yet know
  to *ask* for an independent scorer, because the category has only ever been sold to them as a
  feature of a tool they already use. Independence has commercial value only once (i) the firm
  understands its intake tool's score is marking its own homework, and (ii) an *authority* exists
  that makes "independently verified" a legible, askable-for thing (a published benchmark, a named
  standard, a signed attestation the firm can hand its malpractice carrier). We do not yet have
  that authority built. Whoever builds it first *owns the definition of independence in this
  category*, the way Moody's owns "investment grade." The threat is not Supio; it is a well-funded
  new entrant claiming the independent slot, or the slot simply never becoming salient because no
  one taught the market to value it. **That is why the practical window is 6–12 months, not 24–36:
  the structural moat is safe, but the land-grab for the authority position is now.**

**One-line answer to "how durable and for how long":** *The independence wedge is structurally
uncopyable by any current competitor for as long as they remain bundled (2–3 years minimum), but
its commercial value is perishable — we have roughly 6–12 months to convert the structural
uniqueness into a legible authority position (benchmark + named standard + signed attestation)
before a purpose-built independent entrant or market inertia closes the land-grab.*

**What would collapse the window faster (name the risk):**
- A funded startup launches as "the independent rating agency for PI intake." (Highest-impact,
  currently unseen — tripwire #8.) A pre-revenue solo cannot out-fund it; we can only out-*first*
  it and out-*credibility* it (Yang, published methodology, the CIPA-safe benchmark).
- Supio/Eve stand up a **separately-branded "assurance" or "audit" arm** to *simulate*
  independence (legally still a bundle, but it muddies our differentiator in a demo). Counter is
  the structural test, said plainly: same parent, same P&L, still sells you the intake engine.
- The market keeps buying on feature/price and never prices independence at all — in which case
  the moat is real but worthless, and the correct move is to compete on the develop-queue/SLA
  operational value (the conveyor) with independence as trust-glue, not headline. This is the
  quiet base-rate risk and the reason product depth (B-016/017) must ship alongside the authority
  assets (B-006/B-001), not after them.

## 4. THE DEFENSIBILITY THESIS (what actually compounds)

Independence alone is a *claim*. It becomes a *moat* only when it is welded to three assets that
each take calendar time to accrue and that a bundled vendor structurally will not build. The thesis
is that the moat is the **stack**, not any single layer:

1. **Structural independence (the lock).** Flat monthly fee, no voice agent, no demand product, no
   case-management labor, no CRM. There is nothing downstream the score could flatter. This is the
   Moody's position and the only competitor-proof layer. It is free to hold and impossible for a
   bundled incumbent to copy — but on its own it is just a sentence.

2. **Published methodology + a false-alarm rate (legibility).** The signal-detection move (ROC;
   publish the false-alarm rate as a trust act, seed insight). A rating no one can inspect is a
   vibe; a rating with a published rubric, tiered confidence (BI-RADS-style), and an honest error
   rate is an *instrument*. Bundled vendors will not publish a false-alarm rate on a score that
   sells their own voice agent — the incentive runs the other way. This is where B-006 (the
   CIPA-safe benchmark) and the Calibration & Honesty page do double duty.

3. **The signed, staked attestation (the un-SaaS-able layer).** Austin's speech-act model: a named
   human (Analyst of Record; Yang as methodology reviewer) *stakes* their credibility on the
   finding, cited to a transcript span ("no citation, no claim"). This is the difference between a
   credit *tool* and a credit *rating* — a dashboard cannot sign its name. No incumbent can
   replicate a signed independent opinion without hiring an independent human and giving them
   authority to indict the vendor's own tool, which no vendor will do. This is the deepest layer
   and the one most native to our DNA.

4. **The develop-queue / SLA conveyor (the operational value that survives commoditization).** The
   Eve/Supio scores *diagnose*; none publicly ships a tracked worklist of unresolved case-making
   questions on an SLA clock [inference from absence, still true]. When "we score the call" is
   worthless because everyone does it, "every unresolved value-determining question is worked to
   resolution on a clock, and here's the ledger" is the thing the firm actually pays to keep.
   Independence is the trust-glue; the conveyor is the recurring utility. Ship both (B-016/017).

**The compounding claim:** each of these four gets *more* valuable as the field crowds with bundled
scorers, not less — because a market full of vendors grading their own homework is precisely the
market that comes to need an independent second opinion. The crowding that killed the feature wedge
is the same crowding that makes independence salient. Our job is to be the legible, credentialed,
already-published independent when that need arrives — which is the 6–12-month land-grab.

**The uncomfortable corollary (Ali's honesty rule):** if we are NOT going to build layers 2–4 fast
(benchmark, published false-alarm rate, signed attestation, conveyor), then layer 1 is not a moat
and we are a feature company competing on price against firms that raised $150M and $103M this
year. The moat is real but it is *earned*, not *owned*. The competitive scan does not tell us to
relax; it tells us the authority assets (B-006 → B-001, and the attestation/standard) just moved
from "months-to-pay-off nice-to-have" back toward the critical path — because the window to claim
independence is closing while the incumbents publish solo case studies.
