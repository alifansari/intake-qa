## 0. The model facts under review (stated precisely, because the analysis turns on them)

1. **Pricing is a flat MONTHLY subscription.** Core ~$2,500/mo; Pro ~$5,000/mo. (compliance
   §I.)
2. **The fee is OUTCOME-AGNOSTIC.** It is **never** a percentage of recovery, **never**
   per-case / per-signed-case / per-settlement, and **never** tied to the firm's revenue or to
   the fees of any specific matter. The subscription is the same whether the firm signs zero
   cases or a hundred.
3. **Intake QA is an independent scorer / software-and-analysis vendor to LAW FIRMS (B2B).**
   The customer is the firm. Intake QA scores the firm's **own** inbound intake calls and helps
   the firm re-engage the firm's **own** leaked leads.
4. **Intake QA does NOT steer or refer claimants** to any attorney, does **not** contact
   claimants directly, and does **not** participate in acquiring clients for a fee. It is the
   opposite vector from a "runner/capper": it never sends a claimant to a lawyer.
5. **"Analyst of Record"** (Ali) is an independent evaluator who signs a staked attestation —
   **not** a fee participant in the firm's cases (compliance §I).

If any of these facts is not actually true in a given contract or piece of copy, the analysis

## 1. AB 931 (2025) — "anything of value for recommending/securing an attorney's services" + out-of-state fee-sharing

### The prohibition (VERIFIED — primary source)

AB 931 (Kalra), *State Bar Act: consumer legal funding*, approved by the Governor **Oct 10,
2025**. Two operative pieces matter to us:

- **(a) The "anything of value" prohibition.** AB 931 provides that **"an attorney shall not
  compensate, promise, or give anything of value to a person for the purpose of recommending
  or securing the services of the attorney or the attorney's law firm,"** subject to
  enumerated exceptions. This mirrors — and now backs with statute — the language already in
  **Rule of Professional Conduct 7.2**.
  *(VERIFIED: operative language quoted from the Senate Judiciary Committee analysis of AB 931,
  https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2025-06/ab-931-kalra-sjud-analysis.pdf. The exact
  new Business & Professions Code section number carrying this clause is **UNCERTAIN from the
  sources reviewed** — a question for Yang, §1-Q1.)*

  **Enumerated exceptions (VERIFIED — quoted from the SJUD analysis):** an attorney may
  (a) *"pay the reasonable costs of advertisements or communications permitted by Rule 7.2 …
  or any successor rule,"* (b) *"pay the usual charges of a legal services plan or a qualified
  attorney referral service operating in accordance with the rules established by the state
  bar,"* and (c) *"pay for a law practice operating in accordance with Rule 1.17"* (plus
  reciprocal-referral arrangements per the Rules).

- **(b) The out-of-state fee-sharing prohibition (new B&P §6156).** *"No attorney … shall
  share legal fees directly or indirectly with an out-of-state alternative business
  structure"* (an entity providing legal services while allowing nonattorney ownership/
  management/decision-making) unless narrow conditions apply. **This applies to contracts
  entered into on or after Jan 1, 2026, and §6156 is repealed as of Jan 1, 2030.**
  Penalty (§6156): **statutory damages of $10,000 per violation or three times actual damages,
  whichever is greater**, plus injunctive relief, fees/costs, and State Bar discipline.
  *(VERIFIED: B&P §6156 text via California Legislative Information / public.law and Justia;
  penalty per the SJUD analysis, same source.)*

### Mapping our structure to AB 931

- **Against the "anything of value" clause:** the clause binds the **attorney** and targets
  payments made **"for the purpose of recommending or securing"** the attorney's services —
  i.e., paying someone to send business to the lawyer. Intake QA is paid **by the firm** to
  **score the firm's own calls and re-engage the firm's own leaked leads**; Intake QA is not
  paid **for recommending or securing** the firm's services to anyone, and does not steer or
  refer claimants (fact 4). The firm's payment to Intake QA looks like a payment for **QA /
  software / analysis services**, which parallels the Rule 7.2 exception for the *reasonable
  costs of permitted advertisements/communications and ordinary vendor charges* — not a
  bounty for client acquisition.
- **Against §6156 (out-of-state fee-sharing):** Intake QA is **not** an "alternative business
  structure," is **not** sharing **legal fees**, and receives a **flat subscription** rather
  than any share of legal fees (facts 1–2). §6156 is about attorneys funneling legal fees to
  nonlawyer-owned out-of-state entities; a flat B2B software/analysis subscription is a
  different animal.

### Preliminary read

- "anything of value" clause: **APPEARS OUTSIDE** — because the fee is not "for recommending
  or securing" and Intake QA never steers claimants. *(Our analysis; Yang to confirm.)*
- §6156 out-of-state fee-sharing: **APPEARS OUTSIDE** — no legal-fee share, not an ABS, flat
  fee. *(Our analysis; Yang to confirm.)*
- **Residual risk / UNCERTAIN:** the phrase **"securing … services"** and the phrase
  **"indirectly"** in the fee-sharing clause are the soft spots. If any copy or contract term
  could be read as Intake QA being paid to *generate signed clients* (rather than to *score
  and improve the firm's own process*), the "outside" read weakens. Our recovered-lead
  re-engagement feature is the place to look hardest — see §1-Q3.

### Questions for Yang (AB 931)

- **Q1.** What is the exact new B&P section number carrying the "anything of value"
  prohibition, and does its precise text differ from Rule 7.2 in any way that matters to us?
- **Q2.** Does a **flat, outcome-agnostic** subscription paid **by the firm** for QA/scoring
  fall cleanly outside "compensate … for the purpose of recommending or securing the services
  of the attorney," or is there a reading where an intake-improvement vendor is "securing"
  services?
- **Q3.** Our **recovered-lead re-engagement** helps the firm re-contact the firm's *own*
  prior leads, with a human at the firm approving every message and **Intake QA never
  contacting claimants**. Does that stay outside "anything of value for … securing services,"
  or does helping convert leads look like being paid to secure clients?
- **Q4.** Confirm we are **not** an "alternative business structure" and **not** "sharing
  legal fees … directly or indirectly" under §6156, given the flat subscription.

## 2. SB 37 (2025) — anti-capping, private right of action, broadened "advertisement"

### The prohibition (VERIFIED — primary source)

SB 37 (Umberg), *Attorneys: unlawful solicitations and advertisements*, approved **Oct 11,
2025**, effective **Jan 1, 2026**. It toughens the anti-"capping"/runner regime and modernizes
attorney-advertising rules. Key pieces (VERIFIED against the Senate Judiciary Committee
analysis, https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2025-04/sb-37-umberg-sjud-analysis.pdf, and
the CalMatters bill page, https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202520260sb37):

- **Runner/capper (B&P §§6151–6152).** *"Runner or capper"* means a person/firm acting **"for
  consideration … as an agent for an attorney or law firm in the solicitation or procurement
  of business."* §6152 makes it unlawful to act as a runner/capper or to solicit business for
  attorneys in specified settings. SB 37 amends this article (the analysis references
  §§6151, 6152, 6153, 6156, 6157, 6157.2).
- **Private right of action.** SB 37 *"would additionally authorize any person to bring a civil
  action for a violation of these provisions,"* for statutory damages, attorney's fees,
  injunctive relief, and other relief.
- **Civil penalty range (VERIFIED).** A **minimum of $5,000 up to a maximum of $100,000 per
  violation, or three times** the amount at issue (per the SJUD analysis text).
- **Broadened "advertisement."** Redefined to cover *"any communication … through any written,
  recorded, or electronic means … that provides information concerning a lawyer or the
  lawyer's services **for the purpose of encouraging individuals to secure the services of the
  lawyer**."* The emphasis on *encouraging individuals to secure the services* points at
  **claimant/consumer-facing** communications.

### Mapping our structure to SB 37

- **Runner/capper (§§6151–6152):** a capper acts **for consideration as an agent for the
  attorney in the solicitation or procurement of business** — i.e., someone who rounds up
  **claimants** and delivers them to a lawyer. Intake QA does the **opposite**: it never
  contacts claimants, never solicits or procures **clients** for the firm, and is paid a flat
  fee to **score the firm's own calls** (facts 1–4). We are not an agent procuring business;
  we are a vendor grading a process.
- **Private right of action:** because the underlying conduct (capping/unlawful solicitation/
  deceptive advertising) does not describe what we do, the new private cause of action has no
  hook against our model **if** the facts hold. It does, however, **raise the stakes** on
  getting the analysis right — any misstep is now privately enforceable at $5,000–$100,000/
  violation.
- **Broadened "advertisement":** our marketing is **B2B — we sell software/analysis to law
  firms, not legal services to claimants.** Our newsletter, mailers, LinkedIn, and 1:1 email
  are not *"encouraging individuals to secure the services of [a] lawyer."* They fall outside
  the redefined "advertisement," which is claimant-facing (consistent with ops/insights.md
  A2). BUT SB 37 also targets **unverifiable claims and misleading guarantees**, which
  reinforces our existing "no citation, no claim" / no-guarantee invariants (compliance §IV/
  §V) for anything a firm might republish.

### Preliminary read

- Runner/capper §§6151–6152: **APPEARS OUTSIDE** — we neither solicit nor procure claimants;
  wrong vector entirely. *(Our analysis; Yang to confirm.)*
- Broadened "advertisement": **APPEARS OUTSIDE** — B2B, not claimant-facing; but tighten copy
  discipline (no unverifiable claims/guarantees). *(Our analysis; Yang to confirm.)*
- Private right of action: **not a separate prohibition**, but a **risk multiplier** — it makes
  the cost of being wrong much higher and invites plaintiff-side testing of novel vendors.
- **Residual risk / UNCERTAIN:** the recovered-lead re-engagement feature again deserves the
  hardest look — could a plaintiff creatively argue that helping a firm re-contact leads is
  "procurement of business"? See §2-Q2.

### Questions for Yang (SB 37)

- **Q1.** Does an **independent QA/scoring vendor** that **never contacts claimants and never
  procures clients** sit entirely outside "runner or capper" (§§6151–6152) as amended?
- **Q2.** Does **recovered-lead re-engagement** (firm re-contacts its *own* prior leads;
  firm-human approves each message; Intake QA never messages claimants) risk being
  characterized as "solicitation or procurement of business" under §6152?
- **Q3.** Confirm our **B2B marketing to firms** is outside SB 37's broadened "advertisement"
  definition (claimant-facing), and identify any copy that could be read as claimant-facing.
- **Q4.** Given the new **private right of action** and **$5,000–$100,000/violation** penalties,
  what is the realistic exposure surface for a novel independent-scorer vendor, and what
  contract/copy language best insulates us?

## 3. Rule of Professional Conduct 5.4 (fee-sharing with nonlawyers)

### The prohibition (VERIFIED — primary source)

**Rule 5.4** — a lawyer/law firm **shall not share legal fees directly or indirectly with a
nonlawyer**, with exceptions. Relevant to us (VERIFIED from the State Bar rule text and
executive summary):

- A nonlawyer employee's compensation **"may not be based on a percentage or share of fees in
  specific cases or legal matters."**
- Firm-wide profit-sharing/retirement plans are allowed but **"compensation may not be tied to
  the fees of specific cases."**
- Payments to a **State Bar-certified lawyer referral service** are allowed if it complies with
  the Minimum Standards and the lawyer complies with Rules 5.4, 7.2, 7.3.

Source: Cal. R. Prof. Conduct 5.4, https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/rules/Rule_5.4.pdf;
executive summary redline: https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/rules/Rule_5.4-Exec_Summary-Redline.pdf

### Mapping our structure to Rule 5.4

The Rule's own line is drawn at compensation **"tied to the fees of specific cases."** Intake
QA's fee is a **flat monthly subscription, not tied to any case's fees, outcome, or the firm's
revenue** (facts 1–2). That is the exact feature Rule 5.4 uses to *permit* nonlawyer
compensation arrangements (e.g., overall-profit plans that are **not** tied to specific-case
fees). A flat vendor subscription is even further from fee-sharing than a permitted
profit-based employee plan, because Intake QA is not sharing in **fees** at all — it charges a
price for a service.

### Preliminary read

- **APPEARS OUTSIDE.** A flat, outcome-agnostic subscription is not "sharing legal fees" and is
  categorically not "tied to the fees of specific cases." *(Our analysis; Yang to confirm.)*
- **This is our brightest line and our strongest ground** — and it is exactly why
  compliance-invariants §I bans, as a hard defect, any copy that hints at "% of recovery,"
  "per case," "success fee," or "we only get paid when you do." The moment pricing language
  drifts toward outcome-tied, this clean read collapses.

### Questions for Yang (Rule 5.4)

- **Q1.** Confirm a **flat, outcome-agnostic B2B subscription** is not "sharing legal fees …
  directly or indirectly" and is not "tied to the fees of specific cases."
- **Q2.** Is there any **volume-tier** structure (e.g., price banded by call volume) that could
  be misread as indirectly tied to case outcomes? (Our tiers are by **call volume**, not
  cases/recovery — please confirm that distinction holds.)

## 4. Rules 7.1–7.3 (advertising / solicitation) — the claimant-vs-firm distinction

### The prohibitions (VERIFIED — primary source)

- **Rule 7.1** — no false or misleading communication about the lawyer or the lawyer's
  services.
- **Rule 7.2** — *"a lawyer shall not compensate, promise or give anything of value to a person
  for the purpose of recommending or securing the services of the lawyer or the lawyer's law
  firm,"* with exceptions (reasonable ad costs; legal-service-plan/qualified-referral charges;
  Rule 1.17 practice purchase; reciprocal referrals with notice; gifts not given in
  consideration of future referrals).
- **Rule 7.3** — *"solicitation"* is *"an oral or written targeted communication initiated by or
  on behalf of the lawyer that is directed to a specific person and that offers to provide … 
  legal services."* It targets **specific individuals** (i.e., prospective *clients*).

Source: State Bar Chapter 7,
https://www.calbar.ca.gov/legal-professionals/rules/rules-professional-conduct/current-rules-professional-conduct/chapter-7-information-about-legal-services

### Mapping our structure to 7.1–7.3

- **Rule 7.2 ("anything of value"):** same analysis as AB 931 §1 — the firm pays Intake QA for
  **QA/scoring**, not **"for recommending or securing"** the firm's services; Intake QA does
  not steer claimants. The parallel Rule 7.2 exceptions (reasonable costs of permitted
  communications; ordinary vendor charges) support the "outside" read.
- **Rule 7.3 (solicitation):** governs a lawyer soliciting **prospective clients**. Intake QA
  solicits **law firms (businesses)**, never claimants (compliance §III). The
  claimant-vs-firm distinction must stay crisp and never blur into claimant-facing
  solicitation.
- **Rule 7.1 (no false/misleading):** binds the lawyer's own communications; for us it maps to
  our own truthfulness invariants (compliance §V) — no unsubstantiated superlatives, no
  guaranteed-results implications, Yang framed only as a **named methodology/compliance
  reviewer**, never as endorsing outcomes or marketing the product.

### Preliminary read

- **APPEARS OUTSIDE** on 7.2 (vendor fee, not a referral bounty) and 7.3 (we solicit firms, not
  claimants). *(Our analysis; Yang to confirm.)*
- **Risk to police, not a prohibition we trip:** copy discipline — any drift into (a) implying
  guaranteed recovery, (b) claimant-facing messaging, or (c) implying Yang endorses results —
  would create real 7.1/7.3/§V problems.

### Questions for Yang (7.1–7.3)

- **Q1.** Confirm a flat QA/scoring vendor fee is outside Rule 7.2's "anything of value for
  recommending or securing" the lawyer's services.
- **Q2.** Confirm our **firm-directed** (B2B) outreach is outside Rule 7.3 solicitation (which
  targets prospective *clients*), and flag anything in our outreach that risks reading as
  claimant-facing.
- **Q3.** Is our framing of you as **"named methodology / compliance reviewer"** (never
  endorsing outcomes, never marketing the product) acceptable under Rule 7.1 and the
  testimonial/endorsement rules?

## 5. B&P §§6151–6152 (capping / running) — the anti-capping backbone

### The prohibition (VERIFIED — primary source)

- **§6151** defines *"runner or capper"* as a person/firm/association/corporation acting **"for
  consideration in any manner or in any capacity as an agent for an attorney or law firm in the
  solicitation or procurement of business."**
- **§6152** makes it unlawful to **act as a runner or capper**, or to **solicit business for
  attorneys** in specified settings; SB 37 toughens the article and adds the private right of
  action and enhanced penalties (§2 above).

Source: B&P §§6151–6152 via California Legislative Information / Justia; SB 37 amendments per the
SJUD analysis (§2 sources).

### Mapping our structure to §§6151–6152

The statutory hinge is **"solicitation or procurement of business"** — delivering **clients/
claimants** to a lawyer for pay. Intake QA:

- never contacts claimants (fact 4);
- never solicits or procures **clients** for the firm;
- is paid a **flat fee** to **evaluate the firm's own intake and help the firm re-engage its
  own leads** (facts 1–3);
- is the **opposite vector** from a capper — it improves how the firm handles the leads the
  firm **already** has, rather than routing new claimants to a lawyer.

### Preliminary read

- **APPEARS OUTSIDE.** We are not an "agent … in the solicitation or procurement of business."
  *(Our analysis; Yang to confirm.)*
- **The single hardest question in this memo:** does **recovered-lead re-engagement** — even
  though the firm owns the leads and a firm-human approves each message — risk being
  recharacterized as helping "procure business," especially now that SB 37 adds a **private
  right of action** and $5,000–$100,000/violation penalties that invite creative plaintiff
  theories? This is **UNCERTAIN** and is the risk we most want Yang to pressure-test. See
  §5-Q1/Q2.

### Questions for Yang (§§6151–6152)

- **Q1.** Does an independent vendor that **never contacts claimants** and only helps a firm
  **score and re-engage the firm's own leads** sit outside "solicitation or procurement of
  business" — even under SB 37's toughened regime?
- **Q2.** What contract language, product guardrails, and copy discipline best keep the
  recovered-lead feature unambiguously **firm-owns-the-lead / firm-human-approves-every-send /
  vendor-never-touches-the-claimant**, so it cannot be recharacterized as capping?
