## 2. Refreshed B2 — funnel + the sensitivity that decides everything

### 2a. What the 2026 benchmarks actually say about OUR motion

Our motion is **not** a self-serve free trial. It is a **founder-led, sales-assisted, structured
pilot**: free Leak Audit on the firm's *own* 10 calls → live owner readout with verbatim
transcript + fee-at-risk → founding-rate flat fee. That maps to the *sales-assisted / structured-
pilot* band, **not** the freemium band. The benchmark bands, cleanly separated:

| Motion | Trial/pilot → paid | Source / label |
|---|---|---|
| Freemium (free tier alongside paid) | median ~4.5% | verified-as-reported (aggregators; ChartMogul 10k+ SaaS) |
| Opt-in free trial, no card | median ~8–14% | verified-as-reported (ChartMogul; growthspree 2026) |
| Sales-assisted trial (rep touches every one) | **<35% / 55% / 70%+** (bottom/median/top quartile) | verified-as-reported (growthspree 2026) |
| Sales-assisted, **$15–35K ACV** (our band) | **55–68%** | verified-as-reported (growthspree 2026) |
| **Structured pilot w/ predefined success criteria** | **40–60%**; **3.2× more likely to convert than open-ended** | plausible-unverified (rework/getmonetizely, vendor) |
| Enterprise POC reality check | **>50% of POCs fail** | plausible-unverified (Sapphire Ventures, VC) |
| Founder-led proposal→close | 25–40%; warm intro 30–50%, cold 2–5% | verified-as-reported (multiple founder-sales sources converge) |

**Verdict on the old 50% assumption:** 50% pilot→paid is the *median for a sales-assisted / paid
structured pilot in our ACV band* — so it is **defensible, not aggressive — CONDITIONAL on the
pilot behaving like a structured, high-commitment pilot.** The catch: **our beta is FREE**
(free-during-beta, NDA-gated, no payment or pre-commit). Free + low-commitment selects for
tire-kickers (decisions.md 2026-07-07 said exactly this) and drags the realized rate toward the
opt-in band. **A free, open-ended pilot plausibly converts 25–40%, not 50%.** The single highest-
leverage move in this whole model is therefore **not pricing — it is putting a commitment device
on the free pilot** (predefined written success criteria + a pre-agreed conversion path/date),
because structured pilots convert ~3.2× open-ended ones. Same free audit, 1.5–3× the yield.

### 2b. Sensitivity: does $1M slip to 18 months if pilot→paid is 33% not 50%?

Hold **audit→pilot at 40%** (insight B2). Target **~28 paying firms**. Pilots and audits needed:

| pilot→paid | Pilots for 28 closes | Audits for those pilots (@40%) | Audit-hours per NET close* | Δ vs 50% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60% (paid structured pilot) | 47 | 117 | ~25 hrs | 0.8× |
| **50% (old base)** | **56** | **140** | **~31 hrs** | **1.0×** |
| 40% | 70 | 175 | ~39 hrs | 1.25× |
| **33% (the flag)** | **85** | **212** | **~47 hrs** | **~1.5×** |
| 25% (free, tire-kicker) | 112 | 280 | ~62 hrs | ~2.0× |

\* Audit-hours/net-close = (audits per close) × ~5 hrs/audit (§3), the dominant acquisition cost.

**Answer: yes.** Because the ramp's rate-limiter is analyst hours spent on audits (§3), and audit
hours/net-close scale ~inversely with pilot→paid, dropping 50%→33% multiplies the acquisition
labor per close by **~1.5×**. If acquisition-hours are the binding ramp constraint (they are —
§3), the time to reach any given firm count stretches ~1.5×: **a 12-month ramp becomes ~18
months.** At 25% (the honest free-pilot risk case) it's **~2×** → ~24 months. This is not a
rounding error; it is the difference between the $1M-in-a-year yardstick landing and missing by
half a year to a year. **Instrument pilot→paid from firm #1; it is the master dial.**

**Reframed timeline honesty (unchanged from B3):** even at 50%, $1M is an **exit run-rate in
month ~12**, not year-1 *booked* revenue. Average recognized ARR across year 1 is far below $1M.
Say this out loud to any founder/investor framing.

## 3. THE BINDING CONSTRAINT — analyst-hours-per-closed-firm and the ceiling

Market size is not the wall (2–3% penetration). Pricing is not the wall (settled, ~28 firms).
**The wall is that one Analyst of Record has a fixed hour budget, acquisition and service draw
from the same budget, and service load grows monotonically with every firm you close.**

### 3a. Hours per firm, bottom-up

| Activity | Hrs | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sourcing + recon (Dream-12 targeting) | ~1–2 | per firm worked |
| Outreach (5-touch hand-sent sequence) | ~1–2 | spread over 14 days |
| **Free Leak Audit (the expensive one)** | **~4–6** | 10 calls × ~15 min personal review (the AoR must *personally* verify each flag — you cannot stake your signature on calls you did not hear; §IV "no citation, no claim" + Austin) + readout prep ~1 hr + live 30–45 min owner readout |
| Pilot onboarding | ~2–4 | CallRail setup call, firm config, first-week support |
| **Ongoing monthly service PER PAYING FIRM** | **~3–6 / month, forever** | review flagged subset + QC + **sign the monthly Missed-Revenue Statement** (staked attestation). Scales with call volume; Pro tier + Spanish QA push toward the top of the band |

**Acquisition hours per net close** (at 50% pilot→paid, 40% audit→pilot): ~5 audits/close ×
~5 hrs + outreach ~7.5 + onboarding ~3 ≈ **~35 hrs to acquire one paying firm.** At 33% it rises
to **~51 hrs** (the §2b 1.5×).

### 3b. The ceiling — where it breaks

Assume a solo founder-analyst can protect **~120 productive desk-hours/month** for this desk
(generous: MEMORY notes ~45% of a small-firm principal's time is admin, and this founder is also
building product). Two loads compete for those 120 hours:

- **Service load** = ~4 hrs/firm/month × N firms  (monotonically increasing, never stops)
- **Acquisition load** = whatever's left, converted to closes at ~35 hrs/close

**Serve-only ceiling** (zero acquisition): `N = 120 / 4 = ` **~30 firms** at 4 hrs/firm-mo
(range **20 firms @ 6 hrs** to **40 firms @ 3 hrs**).

**Sustainable acquire-AND-serve wall** (the real one): to keep closing even ~1 firm/month you
must reserve ~35 hrs/month for acquisition, leaving `120 − 35 = 85` hrs for service →
`85 / 4 ≈ ` **~21 firms** before acquisition starves. Quality strain (rushed reviews, thinner
attestation) begins earlier, around **~15 firms**. So:

```
solo founder-analyst, staked-attestation quality held constant:
  ~15 firms  → quality strain begins (review time/firm compresses)
  ~20 firms  → acquisition starves (all hours consumed keeping current firms)
  ~30 firms  → absolute serve-only ceiling (and you can no longer add anyone)
  $1M line   = ~26–31 firms  →  ABOVE the sustainable solo wall
```

**Conclusion (aggressive, then the risk): $1M ARR is not reachable by a single Analyst of Record
at defensible attestation quality.** The one-analyst desk asymptotes around **~20 served firms
while still acquiring**, ~30 as a frozen serve-only ceiling. The ~26–31 firms that $1M needs sit
*at or above* the frozen ceiling — meaning even if you stopped selling the day you hit 30, you'd
be at 100% utilization with zero slack, and one bad month (a churn, a Pro firm with 2× call
volume, a Spanish-heavy firm) drops you below the line.

### 3c. What breaks it — and the deep tension worth naming

The bottleneck is **the moat**. The independent-scorer edge (insight D1) is a *signed, staked
attestation* — a named human independently stands behind each score, the difference between a
credit *tool* and a credit *rating*. That signature is exactly what a junior analyst cannot
produce and a dashboard cannot replicate. **So the property that makes Intake QA uncopyable
(personal attestation) is the property that makes it un-scalable past one analyst's review
capacity.** Three specific break-modes:

1. **Service-hour creep.** Pro tier's deeper workflow, high-call-volume firms, and Spanish QA
   (double-language review) each push service from 4 → 6 hrs/firm-mo, dropping the ceiling from
   ~30 to ~20.
2. **Acquisition/service cannibalization.** They share one pool; existing firms win by default
   (you don't drop a paying client to run an audit), so acquisition is the residual and growth
   self-decelerates — the ramp is inherently S-shaped and flattens near ~20.
3. **The attestation can't be delegated without diluting the moat.** Hire a junior to do reviews
   and the AoR is signing work they didn't do — the staked-words guarantee (§IV) weakens, which
   is the one thing the pricing power rests on.

### 3d. Relief valves (the scale path — name it, plan it)

- **Adopt the literal Moody's structure**: junior analysts do first-pass review; the **Analyst of
  Record signs the committee opinion after spot-check + methodology attestation**, not after
  hearing every call. This *preserves the signature* (the AoR still stakes credibility on the
  published methodology + sample) while distributing labor. It is the historically-proven way a
  rating shop scales attestation. **This is a hire — i.e., it is past the one-analyst ceiling and
  should be the explicit trigger at ~firm 15–18.**
- **Product lane already helps**: the conveyor's surfaced citations (B-016) cut review-time/call;
  every minute shaved off the ~15-min/call review directly raises the ceiling. Prioritize
  review-time reduction as a *unit-economics* feature, not just UX.
- **Cap the founding cohort deliberately** at what one analyst can serve WITH acquisition headroom
  (~12–18 firms), and treat crossing ~20 as the hard trigger for Analyst #2. The pricing
  one-pager's founding pool of ≤10 is comfortably inside this — good; do not raise it without an
  Analyst-of-Record #2 plan.
- **Price/scope Pro to reflect its true service hours** (already 2× Core in dollars — verify it's
  also ≤2× the hours, or Pro silently subsidizes churn of your own time).

## 4. The two instrumentation metrics (exact definitions — instrument from firm #1)

Insight B2: pilot→paid and audit→pilot are "the two numbers that decide everything." Exact,
cohort-based, right-censored definitions so the goalposts never move:

### Metric 1 — Pilot→Paid Conversion Rate (the master dial)
- **Numerator:** count of founding-cohort engagements that execute a signed flat-fee agreement
  **AND have a first invoice issued** (Table C founding rate) within **45 days** of the pilot's
  scoped-audit readout date.
- **Denominator:** count of founding-cohort engagements whose Leak Audit readout completed in the
  same **readout-month cohort**, excluding firms still inside their 45-day decision window
  (right-censor — never inflate by counting an undecided firm as a loss, never by counting a
  verbal-yes as a win).
- **Grain:** by readout-month cohort. **Count only on first invoice**, never on "they said yes."
- **Alarm thresholds:** ≥50% = model on track; 33–49% = ~18-month slip, add a pilot commitment
  device (§2a); <33% = the free-pilot tire-kicker failure mode — stop and fix selection before
  scaling audits.

### Metric 2 — Audit→Pilot Conversion Rate (the upstream gate)
- **Numerator:** firms that begin a scoped pilot engagement — **NDA executed AND calls connected/
  committed for scoring** — within **30 days** of a completed Leak Audit readout.
- **Denominator:** firms that received a **completed** Leak Audit readout in the readout-month
  cohort. A readout is "completed" only when the owner has seen the fee-at-risk on their own calls
  (an audit delivered but never walked-through does not count in either place).
- **Grain:** by readout-month cohort. Instrument reply-by-channel upstream of this so a low rate
  can be traced to audit quality vs targeting.

### Metric 3 (operational, the ceiling instrument) — Analyst-Hours-per-Closed-Firm + Service-Hours
- **AHCF (acquisition):** total AoR hours logged to sourcing + outreach + audits + onboarding in a
  period ÷ net new paying firms closed in that period. **Ceiling flag: watch the trend, not the
  level** — if AHCF is rising month over month while N grows, service is cannibalizing acquisition
  (the §3b wall approaching).
- **OSHFM (service):** total AoR hours logged to serving existing paying firms (review + QC +
  statement signing) ÷ active paying firms, per month. **Hard trigger: when `OSHFM × N` exceeds
  ~70% of available desk-hours, hire/insource Analyst #2 — do not wait for 100%, quality dies
  first.** Derived ceiling to post on the scoreboard each month: `N_max = H_available / OSHFM`.

*(Metrics 1 & 2 are the "two that decide everything" per the brief; Metric 3 is the ceiling
instrument the binding-constraint analysis requires — track all three.)*

## 5. Biggest risk (flagged, not smoothed)

**The free beta and the unit-economics are in tension, and if unresolved the model quietly
converts at ~30% instead of ~50% and the $1M ceiling is a wall you cannot see coming.** Two forces
compound:

1. **Free, open-ended pilots convert below the sales-assisted benchmark** (8–25% opt-in band vs
   55% sales-assisted band). The pricing decision chose free-during-beta to protect WTP data and
   recruit "help me make this great" firms — a defensible call — but it *costs conversion* unless
   the pilot carries a commitment device (written success criteria + pre-agreed conversion date).
   Without it, plan for ~35% pilot→paid and an ~18-month $1M, not 12.
2. **Even at a good conversion rate, the ~26–31 firms $1M needs exceed the ~20-firm sustainable
   solo-analyst wall.** The moat (personal staked attestation) is the bottleneck. **$1M is
   structurally a ≥2-Analyst-of-Record number.** If Ali models $1M as a solo-founder outcome, the
   plan is wrong by construction — either quality (the moat) or the timeline (the yardstick) gives.

**The honest synthesis:** the two dials that decide the *timeline* (pilot→paid, audit→pilot) are
knowable and improvable from firm #1 via a pilot commitment device. The dial that decides the
*ceiling* (attestation-bound analyst hours) is a structural fact that caps a solo desk at ~20
firms — so $1M requires deciding, now, that firm ~15–18 triggers the Moody's-style junior-review +
signing-AoR build-out. Neither dial is TAM or price. Instrument all three; pre-commit the Analyst
#2 trigger.
