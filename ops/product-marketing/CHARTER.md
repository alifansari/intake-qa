## What Product Marketing owns (the boundary)

Product Marketing owns **what the product IS in the buyer's mind and why it's worth a premium.**
It is the source-of-truth layer every other GTM chat pulls from:

- **Category & positioning** — the frame ("closing agent, not receptionist"), the category
  name, the point of view / manifesto, the villain, the reference class we force the buyer into.
- **The messaging house** — value pillars → proof → one-liners → persona messaging → tagline.
- **Premium-price justification** — the *narrative* that makes a premium flat price feel cheap
  (NOT the price table — that's Pricing §I.13; NOT the offer mechanics — that's the offer doc).
- **Competitive contrast** — the "vs. answering service / vs. receptionist AI / vs. a human hire"
  reframes (battlecards themselves live in §I.16 Competitive Intel; PMM owns the *positioning*).
- **Naming & narrative language** — product name, category name, the words we own, the words we
  ban.
- **Launch/messaging governance** — the do/don't language list that keeps every claim
  compliance-clean (§IV "no citation, no claim"; §I no fee-splitting).

**Not owned here (hand-off lanes):** demo choreography (§I.1 Direct Sales), outbound sequences
(§I.2 SDR), demand-gen/paid (§I.5), SEO content production (§I.6), trust/compliance publishing
(§I.8), pricing numbers (§I.13), battlecards (§I.16), case studies/evidence production (§I.18).
PMM sets the *positioning* those lanes execute against; it does not do their production work.

Toward **$1M ARR in one year** (`ops/OPERATING-PROTOCOL.md`). PMM moves it through **price
power and message-market fit**:

- **Price realization** — % of list captured; premium held vs. discounted (positioning is the

- **Category ownership** — do prospects/press/partners describe the market in our words?

## The compliance envelope (non-negotiable — inherited, verbatim in spirit)

The premium comes from POSITIONING, not from a risky price model. These rails are a feature of
the pitch, not a tax on it:

1. **No per-signed-case / outcome-tied / %-of-recovery pricing or language** (Rule 5.4
   fee-splitting; `compliance-invariants` §I). The premium is a **flat** premium. Every value
   narrative earns the price by *reference class* (a revenue-producing closer vs. a cost-center
   receptionist), never by promising a cut of cases. Even the category name
   "signed-case-as-a-service" is a *descriptor of function*, never a *pricing mechanic* — flag
   this line every time it's used.
2. **No unsupported claims (§IV — "no citation, no claim").** No "X% close lift," "signs better
   than humans," "only Spanish intake," or "#1/best/guaranteed" until a measured/audited basis
   exists. We sell the **mechanism** and the **cost of the leak**, not an unproven outcome number.
   Third-party stats (speed-to-lead, missed-call rates, Hispanic-market size) must be cited to a
   real, named, dated source and framed as *industry data*, never as our result.
3. **Honest ship vs. sold vision.** The deployed product is **book-human-default qualifier +
   opt-in document-delivery e-sign** with **attorney-in-the-loop** (see
   `closer-book-human-default-decision-2026-07-12.md`). Positioning may sell the closing *vision*
   and the *direction*, but must never imply autonomous live-signing of real injured callers as
   *shipping behavior*. "Closing agent" describes the job-to-be-done and the roadmap; the ship is
   named honestly wherever a prospect could act on it.
4. **Consent-first, inbound-only, AI self-disclosure, no legal advice, human escape hatch** are
   product defaults AND positioning assets — they DISARM the ethics objection, so we lead with
   them, not bury them.
5. **Spanish parity is a wedge, not yet a parity claim** — route Spanish as vision/differentiator;
   no published "parity" claim until the four-fifths audit exists.

2. Check `ops/backlog.md`, this folder's `backlog.md`, and `ops/decisions.md` for anything new

   the canonical message.

   next frontier rather than manufacturing busywork.
